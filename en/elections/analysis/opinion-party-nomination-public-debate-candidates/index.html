After parties choose, public debate should begin

Our view: nomination should open a programme to public scrutiny rather than close questions inside an organization.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

Kayimit News reports Debout Patriotes selecting Nestor by consensus in November 2026. Vant Bèf Info reports Viktwa’s internal primary for Wilner Joseph in September. Different mechanisms can select an organization’s standard-bearer. They do not replace the public election deciding who governs. [1] [2]

A party can explain its choice. Citizens can ask what that choice offers: first priorities, available funding and verifiable outcomes. Applause in a hall cannot answer those questions.

We support debates with comparable questions and publicly accessible answers. A promise of more police should explain training and cost; more schools should explain teachers and financing. A consistent principle can examine different plans.

That does not require invented scores declaring a winner. It requires sources, clear answers and room to verify claims. Nomination begins the work of making an evidence-based case. A party may authorize someone to represent it; the public must be able to assess what that mandate offers.
