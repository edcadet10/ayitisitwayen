Etzer Émile and the Court of Auditors: what limits should a president accept?

His 2020 criticism opens a question about power: should scrutiny that restrains an opponent remain when you take office?

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In November 2020, Etzer Émile strongly criticized a change affecting the consultative role of the Superior Court of Auditors and Administrative Disputes. Juno7 reported that he considered it an attack on democracy and the rule of law. RNDDH’s Pierre Espérance also warned about scrutiny of public contracts. [1]

The same article recorded a different legal interpretation. Specialist Claudie Marsan argued that the Constitution did not give the court authority to approve draft contracts and emphasized its audit role. The dispute concerned which checks apply, at what stage and under what legal authority. [1]

Timing matters to the public. Review before a contract is signed can prevent a problem. An audit after spending can establish what happened and who must answer. One does not automatically perform every function of the other. This is an analysis of oversight design, not a judicial ruling on the decree.

For Émile as a candidate, the test is to specify what checks his administration would accept before contracting, after expenditure and when a report implicates its allies. A 2020 statement does not establish his current legal programme. It supplies a basis for seeking a verifiable commitment.
