Etzer Emile vs Joanas Gué: buying local means paying producers on time

Using food purchases to support local production was on the table in 2010. This comparison follows the path from contract to delivery and payment.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Selling to the state can open a market for a producer. But paying for seed, workers and transport before receiving payment can also create a large borrowing need. That is a policy problem for local purchasing, not an allegation about a particular contract.

In March 2010, after the earthquake, agriculture minister Joanas Gué advocated directing more food assistance toward Haitian purchases. Métropole documents his position then. It does not establish how many purchases his appeal caused or all their results. [1]

Emile proposes local purchasing for school meals. His economic programme promises 30% of public contracts for Haitian small businesses and payment of state suppliers within 60 days, with automatic late-payment interest. These are campaign commitments, not rules presented as already implemented. [2] [3]

An existing experience deserves examination without automatic credit to either candidate: in June 2026, a WFP official reported its school-meal programme reaching 650,000 children, with nearly three-quarters of meals locally produced. These are WFP’s programme figures, not every Haitian canteen or proof of a direct link to Gué’s 2010 appeal. [4]

Dates can separate an idea from a functioning market: who signs the order, who confirms delivery and when does payment arrive? Small producers need a route that does not require financing a two-month wait. Otherwise a policy intended to help them may be easier for firms that already have capital to use.
