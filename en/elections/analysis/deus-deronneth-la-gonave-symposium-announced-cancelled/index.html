Deronneth’s symposium trail: the cancellation belongs beside the invitation

A later notice changes what the earlier publicity can prove. Other meetings cannot silently replace the cancelled event.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In July 2024, Deronneth’s account promoted a communal-governance symposium at Anse-à-Galets, La Gonâve. The publicity named a discussion of local-government funds and announced speakers. [1]

On July 19, the account published an organiser’s notice cancelling that day’s event at Nyflog. The organiser said the reasons were beyond its control and that it was working on a new date. [2]

That later notice changes the record. An invitation and a speaker card cannot be counted as a completed July 19 meeting. The explanation for cancellation remains the organiser’s statement; this review establishes no separate cause.

An August post presents a later symposium at La Vallée de Jacmel. Its images support a public-meeting sequence. They do not establish that the cancelled La Gonâve event was rescheduled there or that both events occurred. [3]

For a candidate who promotes decentralisation, the next useful evidence concerns participation and results: who attended, what was proposed, what it cost and what local authorities implemented. Following an announcement through its cancellation is part of measuring a record fairly.
