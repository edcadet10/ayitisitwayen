Martelly and gangs: what the UN report says about intermediaries

The 2023 report describes money, weapons and people connecting them. Each link needs its own evidence.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A politician need not appear in a photograph with a gang leader for a relationship to matter. One mechanism described in the UN experts’ 2023 report on Michel Martelly is alleged contact through intermediaries, foundations and members of his close-protection team. [1]

In paragraphs 73 and 74, the experts report support for gangs including Base 257, Village de Dieu, Ti Bois and Grand Ravine through money or firearms. They describe Base 257 as an instrument for preventing anti-government demonstrations in Pétion-Ville, especially from 2014. [1]

The report cites Arnel Joseph’s account of contact with an intermediary in Martelly’s protection unit, and a video in which Ti Lapli discusses police rifles allegedly supplied to gang leaders. These are elements presented by the report; we do not claim independent access to every underlying confidential testimony. [1]

This makes the question more precise than whether he knows a gang leader: who supplied resources, who delivered them, for what purpose and on whose instructions? An investigation needs names, dates and material records at each step.

An expert report identifying these relationships warrants serious scrutiny. It does not authorize assigning the same responsibility to everyone who worked near Martelly or presenting every allegation as a court conviction.
