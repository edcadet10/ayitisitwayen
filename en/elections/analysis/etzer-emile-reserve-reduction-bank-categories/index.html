Etzer Emile and bank reserves: 40% is not the rule for every currency

The programme proposes lower reserves on gourde deposits. A BRH note shows why bank and currency categories must remain clear.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Someone hearing that bank money will be freed may expect credit to arrive automatically. Available funds and approval of your loan are separate stages. Etzer Emile’s reserve proposal needs to explain the bridge between them.

The campaign proposes lowering required reserves on gourde deposits from 40% to 24% in 2029 and 18% in 2033. It specifies consultation with BRH and monitoring of exchange rates and prices. This is neither an already changed rate nor an instruction BRH has already received. [1]

BRH’s October–December 2025 note shows why wording matters. For money-creating banks, it reports 40% on gourde liabilities and 53% on foreign-currency liabilities; savings and housing banks have 28.5% and 41.5%. These are categories for that period, not one rule for all banks in November 2026. [2]

Required reserves are funds an institution must maintain under central-bank rules. Lowering the requirement can create financial room; it does not choose borrowers, guarantee repayment or remove security risks. Agricultural lending therefore needs a mechanism beyond reducing a percentage.

Voters can ask for a traceable path: which categories change, which currency is covered, who receives credit and what measures show price effects? The programme recognizes monitoring. It still needs to show when a risk would cause the policy to slow or change.
