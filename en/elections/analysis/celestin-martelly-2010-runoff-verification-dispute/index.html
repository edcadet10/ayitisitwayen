Célestin and Martelly in 2010: 234 tally sheets and a changed runoff

The OAS report and criticism of its method show why recalculation differs from a full recount.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

When preliminary results appeared on December 7, 2010, Jude Célestin ranked second and Michel Martelly third. Following verification and challenges, Martelly entered the runoff and Célestin was excluded. The OAS/CARICOM report describes the process. [1]

The mission recommended excluding 234 tally sheets it considered irregular. A tally sheet records a polling station’s results; excluding it can remove many votes together. Document-level corrections can therefore change the order of candidates without recounting every ballot. [1]

The issue was not merely whether some sheets had problems. In their January 2011 analysis of the draft report, CEPR’s Mark Weisbrot and Jake Johnston questioned why the same tests were not applied to every sheet. They also challenged the treatment of missing or quarantined returns. [2]

That criticism does not itself prove Célestin won an election that entitled him to office. The mission’s report does not itself establish that every vote nationwide was checked. These are separate limits that should remain visible together.

For voters, the practical lesson is to ask what documents were excluded, under which rules, and whether every candidate faced the same test. A method must explain both what it corrected and what it left unresolved.
