Security should give citizens protection they can trust

Our view: anger at gang violence requires a state response that protects life and answers for its own use of force.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

Vant Bèf Info reported Miragoâne demonstrators backing Muscadin in November 2025 as protection against gangs. A 2026 BINUH report documented his possible involvement in at least 76 summary executions during 2022–2025. Those are human-rights findings alleging involvement, not a conviction or a count covering every subsequent period. [1] [2]

The demand for safety deserves to be heard without becoming unlimited authorization. Amid fear, an official who acts can appear more credible than institutions offering promises. Popular power still needs rules allowing its actions to be examined.

A serious state must explain arrests, preserve evidence, present cases and account for deaths involving its force. That requires functioning police and courts. Abandoning those stages allows accusation to become punishment before evidence is examined.

Any candidate promising order should answer the same questions: what protects an innocent person from official error, and who investigates complaints against security forces? Accountability is a guarantee for citizens that the power protecting them cannot harm them without answering for it.
