Opinion: count women, then examine the positions

An overall percentage can hide principal–deputy differences. Making them visible gives the debate a fairer foundation.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

We believe debate on women’s participation should count presence and position. Deputy roles matter. But one overall percentage can hide differences in access to principal leadership.

In the late-November-15 snapshot of 11,142 preliminary CEP rows, 30.6% are coded F. Across all mayoral positions the share is 35.7%; among principal mayoral positions alone it is 11.8%. We use CEP’s F/M field, not guesses from names or photographs. [1]

These are not election results, commune counts or a quota-violation ruling. They do not establish that a presidential candidate selected every party nominee. Turning the percentage into such an accusation would exceed the data.

The data do justify a request we support: parties should explain principal-candidate selection, entry barriers and candidate support. Beyond appearing in photographs, women should be able to examine the rules opening or closing doors.

Dominique Dupuy and Savannah Savary deserve the same scrutiny of programmes and records as other candidates. Their participation does not erase local patterns, and those patterns do not judge their personal worth. The focus should be rules and outcomes.
