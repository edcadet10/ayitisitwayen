Opinion: a report’s date is not always the date of violence

Accurate data help protect survivors. Mixing dates can make political debate describe a false change in security.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A survivor may need time before speaking. A service recording a case does not mean the attack happened that day. We believe campaigns using sexual-violence data should preserve that distinction and respect the people behind the figures.

BINUH’s July–September 2026 report says 985 rape victims were documented during the quarter. Of these, 344 experienced the violence within that quarter; the other cases concerned earlier periods. The subset of 344 must not be added to 985 as a separate group. [1]

More reporting may reflect better access, more violence or several changes together. These figures alone cannot separate all causes. Fewer reports do not automatically prove improved security, and more reports do not automatically show worse services.

We would ask candidates to measure access to care, legal support, safe shelter and waiting times. Public data must protect identities and locations requiring confidentiality. Neither a campaign nor an article needs to expose a survivor to ask a useful policy question.

Dominique Dupuy and Savannah Savary, like other candidates, can explain which services they would fund. We do not attribute the reported cases to them. The demand is debate serving people rather than weaponizing numbers stripped of meaning.

Sources and verification limits

  1. BINUH: reporting dates and dates of sexual violence, pages 7, 22–23 and notes 4/29

    BINUH / OHCHR
