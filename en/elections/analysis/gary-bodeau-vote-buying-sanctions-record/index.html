Gary Bodeau: when an appointment vote allegedly had a price

The U.S. Treasury describes payments intended to influence votes in the Chamber of Deputies. Bodeau disputes the allegations.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Someone seeking parliamentary approval for a government appointment should face scrutiny of their ability and plans. In Gary Bodeau’s case, the U.S. Treasury describes another mechanism: money allegedly circulating to secure an appointment outcome. [1]

In its April 5, 2023 action, the U.S. administration says the former Chamber president participated in corruption schemes. It describes payments to Haitian officials to secure votes in 2018 and discussions about payments for another appointment vote in 2019. [1]

For the 2019 episode, Treasury identifies 500–600 million gourdes supplied by businessmen to Chamber members. It does not say this entire amount entered Bodeau’s personal pocket. That distinction matters when describing the allegation. [1]

Bodeau responded publicly after the designation. Gazette Haïti reports that he denied having done anything to deserve it and said he intended to use U.S. and Canadian legal mechanisms to contest the allegations. An announced challenge is different from a documented ruling on it. [2]

The sanction is a U.S. administrative decision, not a criminal-trial conviction. Treasury’s statement that corruption empowers gangs must not become a false claim that a court convicted Bodeau of financing gangs. [1]

The institutional question is clear: if appointments depend on private payments, to whom will the appointee owe favors afterward? Receipts, communications and voting records can test that chain; labels alone cannot.
