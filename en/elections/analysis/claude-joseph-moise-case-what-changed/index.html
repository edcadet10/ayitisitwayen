Claude Joseph and the Moïse case: what changed after 2024

Understanding the case requires following Haiti’s inquiry and the U.S. prosecutions separately, including what happened after the first accusations.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated at home on July 7, 2021. Claude Joseph was serving as acting prime minister. Examining this candidate’s record requires understanding what the courts did after the killing. [1] [2]

A 2024 investigative order referred Joseph and others for criminal trial over alleged complicity in the assassination. But on October 13, 2025, the Port-au-Prince appeals court annulled that earlier order and called for a renewed investigation, according to Vant Bèf Info’s account of the ruling. [3]

That matters: presenting the 2024 order alone would leave a major change out of the story. Annulment is also different from an acquittal after trial. The ruling called for further investigation. [3]

BINUH’s July–September 2026 report confirms further hearings in July and August as part of that supplementary inquiry. The renewed investigation had therefore advanced beyond an announcement. [4]

The United States has separate proceedings. In September 2026, the Justice Department announced the transfer of 18 defendants from Haiti to Miami in a case involving 30 indicted people. BINUH reports that ten people had been found guilty in the United States by September 30. That does not mean every accused person was convicted. [2] [4]

Proceedings continued beyond that date. A November 13 report by Miami Herald journalist Jacqueline Charles describes Christian Emmanuel Sanon testifying in his own defense. It reports testimony, not a verdict. [5]

Charges and convictions concerning others do not prove Joseph participated in the assassination. Any connection to a candidate needs its own evidence. This article puts the procedural stages in order; it does not claim to identify every organizer of the crime.
