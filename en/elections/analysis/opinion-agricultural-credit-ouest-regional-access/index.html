Opinion: an agricultural bank must change where credit reaches

BRH reports 75.64% of credit in Ouest for one quarter. Rural-finance promises need measures of how lending reaches beyond it.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A bank can open with agricultural in its name while a rural producer still lacks timely credit. The institution’s name does not establish where money reaches. That is our test for MPH candidate Munir Mourra’s proposed bank and Etzer Emile’s rural-credit programme. [1] [2]

BRH’s October–December 2025 note assigns Ouest 75.64% of financial-system credit and Nord-Ouest 0.68%. This is not agricultural credit alone. Where a loan is recorded is not necessarily a map of every place its funds serve. The reported concentration nevertheless warrants examination. [3]

We do not infer that a particular borrower was rejected because of their department. The figures establish no discriminatory individual decision. They show why a national promise needs more than a money total: where applications originate, where approvals occur and what the funds finance.

Our view is to map service around the producer: where they apply without repeated journeys, which documents are accepted, decision time and first payment date. There should be measures for applicants lacking easy collateral and a route to challenge refusal.

A new bank or credit fund should publish results by department and activity while protecting personal data. That lets the public distinguish a new name from changed access. A rural plan should not merely reproduce lending already easiest to obtain in the same places.
