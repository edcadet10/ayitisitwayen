Jacky Lumarque in 2015: do not merge two clearance files

A candidate’s name on a decision does not mean it covers every previous role.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An archived CSCCA decision dated June 8, 2015 concerns Jacky Lumarque and the national Route de l’Esclave committee for May 1998–August 2000. It declares he was not a public accountant in that role and lifts potential restrictions on his assets. [1]

The electoral-exclusion dispute involved another file: GTEF, the education and training working group. In July 2015, Pierre-Louis Opont defended the CEP’s position by saying a June 3 certificate came after the document deadline. That is the position reported by Métropole. [2]

The dates and roles differ. Using the Route de l’Esclave decision as if it settled every GTEF question would mislead readers. Equally, exclusion over electoral paperwork is not a conviction for stealing money.

A full reconstruction needs each document’s submission date, covered role and subsequent decision. That separates two questions: did the accounts require clearance, and did the electoral institution correctly apply procedure?

The lesson applies to the coming election too. A certificate should not become approval of someone’s entire life; a deadline dispute should not become a criminal accusation. Start with the file’s identity and the decision’s actual terms.
