Etzer Emile vs René St-Fort: who makes sure the teacher gets paid?

A common curriculum and a larger budget can help schools. Money must also reach the teacher, on the date it is due.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Imagine a school receiving a new curriculum while its teacher goes unpaid. The document may be good; the classroom problem remains. That example connects René St-Fort’s position on teaching to Etzer Emile’s education-financing commitments.

In his May 15, 2026 Vant Bèf Info interview, St-Fort advocated common school curricula and better teaching quality. He criticized inadequate, irregular teachers’ pay. That interview provides neither a budget nor a timetable for correcting the problem. [1]

Emile’s programme promises regular pay, training and rural housing for teachers. It sets recruitment-and-training targets of 6,000 teachers in 2029 and 15,000 in 2033. These are campaign targets; an announced recruitment figure does not establish capacity to pay everyone monthly. [2]

The programme assigns education 16% of public spending actually executed in 2029, then 22% of the national budget in 2033. Those percentages measure different things. A budget authorizes spending; execution records spending that occurs. They cannot form a single progress line without further information. [2]

Voters can ask both for the teacher payroll, the arrears addressed first and the payment dates to meet. For Emile, that tests the means behind the figures. For St-Fort, it connects lesson quality to teachers’ working conditions. A school needs both; a better document cannot replace either.
