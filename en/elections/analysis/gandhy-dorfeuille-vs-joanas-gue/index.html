Gandhy Dorfeuille vs Joanas Gué: after the launch, does the service last?

Electricity and irrigation are different services. Both require tracing money through completed work to the maintenance people need afterward.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An electricity line needs to deliver power. A canal needs to carry water when crops need it. Gandhy Dorfeuille’s and Joanas Gué’s records call for examining what remains after work is announced or inaugurated.

An April 22, 2014 Le Nouvelliste report describes a second electrification phase in Saint-Louis du Sud involving deputy Dorfeuille. The public excerpt gives a Treasury-funded budget of 24 million gourdes, with 18 million already available. The six-million difference is not proof of missing money: a budget, available funds and executed expenditure are different measures. [1]

For Gué, Métropole reported his January 7, 2009 statement as agriculture minister that emergency funds had repaired 115 irrigation systems after the 2008 hurricanes. This is his account, not our audit of each system. A repair total does not establish harvests protected in subsequent years. [2]

A single figure cannot rank the two records. Years, roles and services differ. For Dorfeuille, assessment needs completion records, connections entering service and duration of supply. For Gué, it needs the 115 systems’ locations, repairs and maintenance responsibilities. In both, contracts and payments must connect to a service people can verify.

A failure today does not prove earlier work never occurred: later disasters and administrations can intervene. A candidate’s experience should nevertheless explain how the next project will keep serving the public. Maintenance belongs in the promise, even when it produces fewer photographs than an inauguration.
