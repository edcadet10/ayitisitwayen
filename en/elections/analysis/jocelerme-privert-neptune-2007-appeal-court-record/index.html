Privert and the Neptune record: another step in 2007

The inter-American judgment quotes a decision after the 2006 release. Its jurisdiction discussion is not a collective verdict.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

The La Scierie record does not stop at one release page. A later trail appears in another court: the 2008 Yvon Neptune v Haiti judgment. It shows why appellants, beneficiaries and issues decided must be separated.

Paragraph 58 and footnote 86 quote the Gonaïves appeal decision of April 13, 2007. Privert is named among appellants. The excerpt separates jurisdiction concerning Neptune and four others from renewed investigation concerning other defendants, while reserving the merits. [1]

The four other names are absent from that passage. We cannot assign Privert to a category it does not identify for him. We examine the quotation in the international judgment, not a complete copy of the 2007 decision.

In paragraphs 75–84, the inter-American court examines Neptune’s legal uncertainty and lack of access to a competent court. It addresses state rights violations, not a criminal trial acquitting or convicting Privert over La Scierie. [1]

That changes how the history should be read: a case can produce a real ruling on someone’s rights without answering every allegation about events. Voters deserve both facts rather than a label erasing either one.

Sources and verification limits

  1. Neptune v Haiti: May 6, 2008 judgment, especially paragraphs 58–64 and 75–84

    Inter-American Court of Human Rights · 2008-05-06

    Judgment against the State quotes the April 13, 2007 appeal decision and names Privert among appellants. Neptune’s international remedy is not Privert’s acquittal; the excerpt does not identify four unnamed people in the jurisdiction exception.
