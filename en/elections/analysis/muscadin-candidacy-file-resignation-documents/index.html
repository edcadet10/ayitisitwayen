Muscadin: an incomplete file does not settle a candidacy

A missing-document report, a resignation letter and a registry entry describe three stages. They should not be treated as the same decision.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On October 29, 2026, Juno7 reported that Jean Ernest Muscadin’s candidacy file had been declared inadmissible because it lacked definitive tax declarations for 2025 and 2026 and his resignation letter. The newsroom also said it did not yet possess the official decision document. That limits what can be verified from its report. [1]

Juno7 subsequently reported on November 6 that Justice Minister Patrick Pélissier accepted the resignation Muscadin submitted on October 29. Ministry letter MJSP/SCGe/33 concerns his departure as Miragoâne’s government commissioner. It is not a CEP decision approving his presidential candidacy. [2]

Muscadin also appears under FCN in the CEP registration snapshot reviewed November 14. An entry does not establish that every legal requirement has been satisfied. A registration registry and a final approved-candidate list are different documents. [3]

Missing tax paperwork does not prove unpaid taxes. A resignation letter missing from a file does not prove no resignation occurred. A later letter does not, by itself, establish what electoral authorities possessed earlier.

The official decision, submitted-document list and any correction or appeal would resolve the question. Without them, declaring the candidacy definitively rejected or definitively approved would go beyond the evidence examined here.
