Published comparisons

  • Claude Joseph vs Jocelerme Privert: temporary power and deadlines

    An acting premier in 2021 and a provisional president in 2016 held different offices. Both records raise questions about handing over power.

  • Munir Mourra vs Jude Elie: when private-sector experience seeks state power

    Partnership proposals and construction interests require clear separation between experience and personal benefit.

  • Etzer Emile vs Jude Elie: infrastructure promises and planning experience

    A national programme and a NYCHA role are different evidence. Costs, maintenance and responsibility connect their assessment.

  • Walsonn Sanon vs Michelet Nestor: whose consent counts?

    Rejecting an agreement and winning a nomination measure different things. Both invite clear rules on who participated.

  • Hans Joseph vs Kely Bastien: transparency should leave a record

    One led ULCC, the other the Senate. Their experience calls for different records to assess what they did.

  • Martine Moïse vs Jocelerme Privert: two decisions with different effects

    Restarting an inquiry and releasing a person are different decisions. Neither automatically decides the substance of the allegations.

  • Wilson Jeudy vs Savannah Savary: employee numbers do not establish capacity to pay wages

    A mayor’s statement on municipal cash and an entrepreneur’s employment account require separating available funds, costs and job totals.

  • Jude Elie vs Olivier Barrau: a company name is not an asset declaration

    Entrepreneurial biographies, partner titles and a company balance sheet supply different information. Comparing them requires preserving each document’s limits.

  • Kely Bastien vs Clarens Renois: leaders’ agreement is not every citizen’s mandate

    DELIVRANS nomination and 2015 Protestant negotiations require understanding who could commit whom.

  • Gary Bodeau vs Jocelerme Privert: a vote count is not the entire story of selecting a leader

    A 2016 National Assembly vote and U.S. allegations concerning 2018–2019 appointments are different records of parliamentary power.

  • Savannah Savary vs Martine Moïse: who else do their parties present to voters?

    A woman presidential registrant does not explain the composition of all her party’s registrations. The CEP snapshot provides a bounded starting point.

  • Savannah Savary vs Jacky Lumarque: what remains after an activity about rights?

    Savary’s writing workshops and a research library presented by Lumarque offer different resources to examine without treating an activity as a national outcome.

  • Claude Joseph vs Gary Bodeau: two foreign lists do not mean the same decision

    An entry restriction and financial sanctions require separating the authority, measure, grounds and the person’s response.

  • Wilson Jeudy vs René St-Fort: a displaced family should not disappear from public services

    The 2011 Delmas evictions and 2026 electoral access are distinct records. Both raise how authorities remain accountable to people losing their place of residence.

  • Kely Bastien vs Jocelerme Privert: when provisional power reaches its deadline

    A 2010 position and a 120-day mandate in 2016 raise a shared question: who decides after the deadline?

  • Joanas Gué vs Olivier Barrau: a harvest can be good while its market is closed

    Mangoes barred from export and rainfall-triggered insurance concern different risks within farming life.

  • Munir Mourra vs Jerry Tardieu: partnership does not always mean the same contract

    MPH’s proposed farm-distribution centres and Royal Oasis financing require separating loans, equity and state responsibility.

  • Gandhy Dorfeuille vs Munir Mourra: construction schedules differ from the hours electricity arrives

    Saint-Louis du Sud in 2014 and a Jacmel record in 2026 provide two ways to examine an energy promise’s results.

  • Martine Moïse vs Dominique Dupuy: representing Haiti does not explain every power of an office

    A 2018 health-coordination role and a 2024 ministry require separating public appearances, decisions and accountability.

  • Lucmanne Delille vs Jean Ernest Muscadin: an officer’s rights and limits on power belong together

    A 2020 police-union decision and a 2023 jurisdictional reprimand illuminate two sides of public service.

  • Hans Joseph vs Etzer Emile: oversight must be able to examine their own side

    An economist who criticized constraints on the Court of Accounts and a former ULCC director must explain protections for those scrutinizing their own power.

  • Savannah Savary vs Jude Elie: who controls the business during a presidential term?

    Inherited firms, declared businesses and a partner title require different records. The public issue is power over decisions affecting those interests.

  • Jerry Tardieu vs René St-Fort: speaking with a citizen is not their signature of support

    Constitutional consultation and party membership serve different functions. Both need a record of what the citizen actually agreed to.

  • Etzer Emile vs Jacky Lumarque: who checks employment after the diploma?

    Emile links employer-training support to employment entry. Lumarque’s record allows scrutiny of how training outcomes are measured over time.

  • Munir Mourra vs Jacky Lumarque: a campus needs a budget after construction

    A proposed campus for at least 4,000 students and a rector’s experience raise questions about fees, teachers and the university’s purpose.

  • Jerry Tardieu vs Jude Célestin: after the financing, what assets and services remain?

    A private hotel and public equipment are different records. Both show why financing figures need accounts of what they delivered.

  • Jean Ernest Muscadin vs Munir Mourra: an armored vehicle is not a security budget

    Citizens can buy equipment. A state must fund its use, maintenance and oversight for years. Those responsibilities deserve examining together.

  • Etzer Emile vs Joanas Gué: buying local means paying producers on time

    Using food purchases to support local production was on the table in 2010. This comparison follows the path from contract to delivery and payment.

  • Etzer Emile vs René St-Fort: who makes sure the teacher gets paid?

    A common curriculum and a larger budget can help schools. Money must also reach the teacher, on the date it is due.

  • Dominique Dupuy vs Savannah Savary: the same precision, different rules for money

    A diplomatic-spending decision and an account of family businesses are different records. They help identify what the public needs to assess each candidate.

  • Clarens Renois vs Junior Salomon: what oversight remains when allies hold power?

    A signature on the 2024 pact and an OPL protest in 2025 show why political accountability needs more than ally or opponent labels.

  • Olivier Barrau vs Munir Mourra: an agricultural bank needs an answer when a harvest fails

    Credit, irrigation and insurance belong to the same farming season. They address different problems, but their rules need to work together.

  • Jerry Tardieu vs Jacky Lumarque: a scholarship must identify who pays and who selects

    Both attended a 2020 scholarship launch. That connection does not merge the programmes into one fund or make the financing a personal gift.

  • Claude Joseph vs Etzer Emile: emergency powers and capacity that must endure

    One headed a cabinet after the president’s assassination. The other proposes expanding police. The comparison follows deadlines, oversight and what lasts after the first announcement.

  • Jean Ernest Muscadin vs Michel Martelly: public protection needs answers about power

    A UN report about a prosecutor and U.S. sanctions against a former president are different records. They require distinct evidentiary paths and the same precision.

  • Etzer Emile vs Jerry Tardieu: who chooses the road, and who maintains it?

    A large investment plan and a debate over departmental power meet at one question: how does a national decision keep serving a commune?

  • Etzer Emile vs Olivier Barrau: after a loss, who carries the cost?

    A loan can start or expand an activity. Insurance can help after a shock. Comparing these records means following risk as well as announced money.

  • Michel Martelly vs Etzer Emile: follow school money into the classroom

    A transfer-fee record and two budget targets show why money collected, authorized and spent must remain distinct.

  • Martine Moïse vs Claude Joseph: one killing, different judicial roles

    A survivor and a former prime minister appear at different judicial stages. Election registration neither erases those records nor supplies a verdict on them.

  • Walsonn Sanon vs Clarens Renois: rejecting one pact, signing another

    The agreements and years differ. Their positions allow scrutiny of participation in transitional decisions and how leaders account for those choices.

  • Jude Elie vs Wilner Joseph: an institution’s name does not explain a person’s role

    A NYCHA chart and a report identifying a government adviser provide two starting points. Roles, dates and outcomes need separating before experience can be assessed.

  • Gandhy Dorfeuille vs Joanas Gué: after the launch, does the service last?

    Electricity and irrigation are different services. Both require tracing money through completed work to the maintenance people need afterward.

  • Déus Deronneth vs Marc-Arthur Drouillard: a result is not the whole election story

    One obtained a changed result after challenges. The other announced withdrawal to support Jovenel Moïse. These are different political acts to examine.

  • Kely Bastien vs Junior Salomon: a Senate title does not explain all the power

    Their records raise different questions: how a leader accounts for his decisions, and how he obtains authority to lead.

  • Daniest Adrien vs Ricardo Jean Pierre: a party slate is not its vote total

    PRC has more Senate entries; PANAH has one more deputy entry. The data show participation in the contest without predicting who will win.

  • Savannah Savary vs Olivier Barrau: family businesses and the public interest

    Their business histories explain part of their careers. The public question concerns what they control today and the safeguards they accept for tomorrow.

  • Wilson Jeudy vs Jude Célestin: public works must account for the people affected

    Delmas camp clearances and CNE equipment purchases are different records. They offer two ways to examine authority, resources and public service.

  • Hans Joseph vs Lucmanne Delille: an investigation must reach a judge

    A former ULCC head and a former justice minister held different responsibilities. Their records direct attention to the full justice process, beyond an investigation’s announcement.

  • Jacky Lumarque vs René St-Fort: from the lesson to a student’s life

    Training experience and proposals on teachers’ conditions address different parts of the same chain. Access, learning and what follows all need examination.

  • Etzer Emile vs Munir Mourra: doubling police is not doubling budgets

    Two security proposals use doubling. One counts people; the other counts money. The outcome for citizens requires more than those figures.

  • Etzer Emile vs Moïse Durocher: what counts as a job?

    A small-business loan, temporary employment and a lasting job are different outcomes. Campaign totals need common definitions before comparison.

  • Clarens Renois vs Maxo Joseph: a church agreement does not own anyone’s vote

    They participated in the same political process in 2015. Later records show why alliances need dates and support networks must be distinguished from voters’ rights.

  • Jerry Tardieu vs Gary Bodeau: who controlled access in Parliament?

    The 2019 record raises access to documents. U.S. sanctions against Bodeau raise a separate question about money and influence that must remain distinct.

  • Dominique Dupuy vs Claude Joseph: after diplomatic words, what protection?

    The former foreign ministers bring different records at the same border. A public stance alone does not measure what changed for citizens.

  • Michel Martelly vs Jocelerme Privert: two presidents, different accountability tests

    Martelly’s school promise and Privert’s transitional mandate are different records. Comparing them requires separating money, authority and deadlines.

  • Jerry Tardieu vs Olivier Barrau: business experience must show whom it serves

    A hotel and an insurer received international financing. Comparing the records means following the money to the people it was intended to help.

  • Etzer Emile vs Jean Ernest Muscadin: more police, accountable to whom?

    One offers police recruitment targets. The other has a prosecutorial record that raises questions about the use of force. Security requires examining both.

  • Nestor, Savary and DPA: a launch did not settle every affiliation

    Dated records show why appearing at a political event does not, by itself, establish someone’s eventual party affiliation.

  • Célestin and Martelly in 2010: 234 tally sheets and a changed runoff

    The OAS report and criticism of its method show why recalculation differs from a full recount.

  • Martelly and Bodeau: why foreign sanctions do not automatically settle election eligibility

    Understanding their presence on the registration dashboard requires separating three different decisions.

How we work

Analysis examines what the evidence supports. Opinion makes a clearly labeled editorial argument. We distinguish verified information, our interpretations and open questions. The same sourcing and accuracy requirements apply to every candidate; the initial selection is neither a ranking nor an endorsement.

Ayiti Sitwayen takes responsibility for these publications. The cited sources let readers examine their basis and submit corrections or new evidence.
