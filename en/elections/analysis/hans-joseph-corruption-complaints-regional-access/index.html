Hans Joseph: reporting corruption should not require a trip to Port-au-Prince

Two State documents put citizens’ access at the center of the anti-corruption record.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Imagine a citizen with evidence of a bribe demand who cannot afford travel or internet. A complaint portal can exist without being usable by that person. This is an illustration of the problem, not testimony from someone we interviewed.

In 2021, the ULCC under Hans Jacques Ludwig Joseph acknowledged internet costs and poor access in remote areas as barriers in its OAS submission. Those conditions limited the value of digital tools for informing the public and encouraging reports. [1]

In its March 2024–February 2025 progress report, the State records a Nord-Est ULCC office opening on January 24, 2025. Its stated purpose was to bring complaint and asset-declaration services closer to residents. [2]

The opening is a concrete institutional action. It does not itself establish how many people received service, how many complaints were handled or how long they waited. A ceremony is not an outcome evaluation.

A useful test of Joseph’s presidential proposals is what service a person with little money can obtain from first contact through follow-up. A serious system should protect evidence and the people providing it, with responses that can be tracked. Access is part of justice.
