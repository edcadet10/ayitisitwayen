Wilson Jeudy: what the 2011 camp clearances require us to examine

Security was the rationale for an operation. Families losing shelter had rights too. The contemporary record puts those responsibilities side by side.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In May 2011, more than 300 families were evicted from two Delmas camps, Amnesty International reported. Its account describes municipal workers and police destroying shelters and belongings after the earthquake had left residents homeless. Wilson Jeudy was the mayor. [1]

At the time, Haïti en Marche defended the mayor’s responsibility to protect security and criticized the central State and organizations handling displacement. That competing account matters. A security rationale, however, does not establish that every camp resident is a criminal. [2]

The BAI legal organization announced plans to file a complaint against Jeudy over the operations. That release announces a legal step; it is not a conviction. We did not locate a final decision on that complaint in the records reviewed. [3]

Another detail prevents a blanket accusation. When people threatened Camp Mosayik and neighboring Camp Mormon in September, municipal authorities denied sending them. Amnesty recorded that denial. Those threats and the May evictions were separate events. [1]

For voters today, the issue reaches beyond one operation. When an official clears an area for public security, who checks the authority for doing so? What notice do families receive? Where can they go, and where can they challenge the action? A security policy must explain how it protects lives while preserving rights.
