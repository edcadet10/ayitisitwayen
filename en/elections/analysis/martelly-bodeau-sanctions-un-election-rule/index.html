Martelly and Bodeau: why foreign sanctions do not automatically settle election eligibility

Understanding their presence on the registration dashboard requires separating three different decisions.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Michel Martelly and Gary Bodeau face U.S. sanctions. Yet both names appear on the CEP’s presidential registration dashboard. The apparent contradiction looks simple only if every sanctions list is treated as the same list. [1] [2] [3]

In the October 6, 2026 decree, Article 76’s eighth condition names UN Security Council sanctions. That is a different authority from the U.S. Treasury. Neither Martelly nor Bodeau appears in the official UN file generated November 15. [4] [5]

This does not make U.S. sanctions disappear. A Haitian electoral rule does not erase another country’s measures. Nor does it certify innocence regarding the foreign authorities’ stated grounds.

Article 76 has other conditions and also refers to constitutional requirements. A name on a registration dashboard is not a final determination on every document or requirement. We are not declaring that the CEP must admit or reject either person. [4]

The public therefore faces three separate questions: the grounds for sanctions, the rule the CEP applies to admission, and voters’ judgment after examining the record. Answering one does not automatically answer the others.
