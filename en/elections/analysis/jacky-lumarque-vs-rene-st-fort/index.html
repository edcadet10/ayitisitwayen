Jacky Lumarque vs René St-Fort: from the lesson to a student’s life

Training experience and proposals on teachers’ conditions address different parts of the same chain. Access, learning and what follows all need examination.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A school can open without every child learning. A centre can issue certificates without every young person finding work. Comparing Jacky Lumarque and René St-Fort requires separating institutional names from outcomes for the people attending.

Université Quisqueya’s biography places Lumarque as INFP deputy director in 1984 and director-general in 1987, and says he has been the university’s rector since 2007. This records responsibilities in state vocational training and a private university. His institution’s biography is not an independent evaluation of his performance. [1]

In Vant Bèf Info’s May 15, 2026 interview, St-Fort calls for common curricula and improved teaching quality, criticizing insufficient and irregular teacher pay. He is a lawyer, teacher and PRN leader. The interview establishes the ideas he defended, but supplies no implementation budget or timetable. [2] [3]

These approaches need not be opposed. A system needs basic education and sound training afterward. But there are different tests: Lumarque can show entrants, completions and subsequent work in the trained field. St-Fort needs to explain reliable teacher pay, materials and tracking of teaching days and student progress.

Access connects the two. Who pays, and what happens to a family that cannot afford it? Experience in a prominent institution does not answer that by itself. A common curriculum does not give every school equal resources. The comparison becomes useful when each candidate connects his approach to students served, cost and a way to verify results.
