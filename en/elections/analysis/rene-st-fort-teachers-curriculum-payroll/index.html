René St-Fort: a common curriculum needs the means to teach it

His education proposals connect two issues: what children should learn and the conditions of those who teach them.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In a May 15, 2026 Vant Bèf Info interview, lawyer and teacher René St Fort called for common school curricula and improved teaching quality. He also criticized irregular and insufficient teachers’ pay. A separate March VBI interview identified him as a National Reformist Party leader. [1] [2]

The two ideas are connected. A common lesson list cannot give pupils equal opportunity if teachers lack training, materials or timely pay. Rules about what to teach need a plan for the people delivering it. Otherwise standardization can remain on paper.

Consider two hypothetical schools following the same curriculum. One has teachers throughout the week and books for pupils; the other repeatedly loses teaching days. Both may follow the same document while delivering different opportunities. Teaching days and pupils’ progress therefore matter alongside the curriculum itself.

The interview gives no budget or timetable. That does not invalidate the ideas; it identifies the detail needed next: which schools receive support first, how payroll becomes reliable and which outcomes are measured. That is how a tribute to teachers can become a public programme voters can assess. [1]
