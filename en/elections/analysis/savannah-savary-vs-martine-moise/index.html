Savannah Savary vs Martine Moïse: who else do their parties present to voters?

A woman presidential registrant does not explain the composition of all her party’s registrations. The CEP snapshot provides a bounded starting point.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A campaign can put a woman at the front while other positions present a different picture. For Savannah Savary and Martine Moïse, we examined legislative registrations under HAMAD and GAD. This is a CEP-record calculation, not a support poll or judgment of commitment to women. [1]

In the late November 15 snapshot, HAMAD has seven deputy and three Senate registrations, all ten marked M. GAD has 44 deputy registrations, one marked F, and 22 Senate registrations, two marked F: three F rows among GAD’s 66 legislative registrations, versus zero among HAMAD’s ten. [1]

The size difference matters: ten and 66 are unequal groups. A party code does not establish that its presidential candidate personally selected every name or reveal the complete internal nomination process. Registration is still neither final CEP approval nor an election result.

The data permit questions about nomination authority, whom parties recruited and barriers applicants encountered. Answers could explain selections, unsuccessful applications and access methods. A representation slogan cannot replace that record.

The comparison moves attention from one poster to the team seeking legislative power. It diminishes neither woman’s experience or right to run. It requires the same transparency across positions, regardless of who heads the ticket.
