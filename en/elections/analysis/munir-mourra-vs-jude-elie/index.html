Munir Mourra vs Jude Elie: when private-sector experience seeks state power

Partnership proposals and construction interests require clear separation between experience and personal benefit.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Business experience can help someone understand a project. A president must also choose fair rules for personal businesses, competitors and the citizens paying for services.

The manifesto of Munir Mourra’s MPH proposes agricultural centres through public-private partnerships. That is a proposed model, not a contract already awarded to a specified firm. It does not establish that the candidate would take contracts personally. [1]

Jude Elie’s website describes interests in construction, green energy and petroleum distribution. Million Billion Group presents him as an infrastructure and development-strategy partner. These are website statements, not a verified register of his complete current holdings. [2] [3]

In both cases, the practical issue is protecting public decisions from private interests. Which interests would be declared, who would evaluate bids and how would officials step aside from decisions touching their businesses? These are proposed safeguards, not findings of violations.

A sound contract would let households identify the service owed, price payable and complaint route. That is a more useful debate. Businesspeople do not all have conflicts; rules should make any actual conflict visible.
