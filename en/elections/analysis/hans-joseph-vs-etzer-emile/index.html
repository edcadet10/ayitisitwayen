Hans Joseph vs Etzer Emile: oversight must be able to examine their own side

An economist who criticized constraints on the Court of Accounts and a former ULCC director must explain protections for those scrutinizing their own power.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

It is easy to demand investigation when an opponent governs. A sharper test comes when an inquiry concerns an ally or your decision. Public positions taken by Etzer Emile and Hans Joseph make that question concrete without treating their records as one case.

In 2020, Juno7 reported Emile criticizing changes affecting the Court of Accounts’ control over public contracts. That is his position in the debate, not a judicial decision attributed to him. It creates a standard to revisit when he seeks executive power. [1]

In his July 2026 AyiboPost interview, Joseph linked his departure from ULCC to criticism of the High Court of Justice decree. He had led ULCC since 2020. His claimed causal link remains his statement; chronology alone does not prove the removal’s motive. [2]

The Court of Accounts and ULCC are distinct institutions with different duties. Their independence raises shared questions about appointments, budgets, document access and public reasons for replacing leaders. Protection depending only on presidential goodwill may not survive disagreement.

A concrete commitment from Emile and Joseph would set rules applying even when scrutiny troubles their side. Which records remain accessible, who can review a dismissal and when are findings published? That turns criticism of previous power into limits accepted on their own.
