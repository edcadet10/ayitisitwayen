Salomon and OPL: opposing a council that included another party leader

A 2025 protest in Les Cayes shows why a party does not always speak with one voice. Connections and disagreements both need documenting.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On July 29, 2025, Rezo Nòdwès reported police dispersing an anti-CPT demonstration in Les Cayes involving former senator Jean-Marie Junior Salomon and OPL members. Its principal quoted participant, Hervé Saintilus, also criticized Edgard Leblanc Fils, another OPL figure serving on the council. [1]

The April 2024 agreement clarifies the connection: it names Leblanc as the collective of January 30 political parties’ council representative. That does not make Salomon a CPT member or establish his approval of Leblanc’s decisions. A nominating coalition and every individual in one party are not the same actor. [2]

Similarly, Saintilus’s accusations about privilege and personal interests are not an audit of Leblanc. They document a political disagreement expressed through protest. Formal party decisions would clarify who had authority to speak, which positions were adopted and how disagreement was handled. [1]

For Salomon as a candidate, neither assigning him every OPL colleague’s decision nor treating protest as proof of severed political ties would be sound. Positions, mandates and actions allow voters to trace accountability.
