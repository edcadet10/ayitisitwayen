Jovenel Moïse’s assassination: the trail of a US$175,000 loan

The Miami trial summary describes pandemic-relief proceeds entering the plot’s financing. The trail requires specific people and accounts.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A political assassination requires people, weapons and money. In Jovenel Moïse’s case, following money can replace sweeping rumours with a specific operation: who lent it, which account carried it and what it financed?

According to the U.S. Justice Department after the May 8, 2026 verdict, Walter Veintemilla agreed from April 2021 to finance the plot through a US$175,000 loan. Proceeds came from other people’s PPP and EIDL pandemic-relief funds, through an account controlled by a co-conspirator. [1]

PPP here means the U.S. pandemic Paycheck Protection Program, not a public-private partnership. Programme proceeds entering a plot do not establish that the government creating the programme ordered the assassination.

Martine Moïse was seriously wounded in the attack. This account does not identify her as financing the loan. We lack the full banking exhibits needed to name every relief recipient; that gap cannot be filled with candidates’ names. [1]

The trail exposes a specific danger: money intended for a public purpose can be redirected through intermediaries. Stronger follow-up would connect each transfer to a person, date and use. That gives political scrutiny something verifiable.
