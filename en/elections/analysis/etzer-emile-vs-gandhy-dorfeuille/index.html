Etzer Emile vs Gandhy Dorfeuille: from capacity to electricity at home

A national target and a local launch require different service measures. Insecurity adds a continuity question.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

For a household, electricity is what reaches a socket when needed. National capacity and a local line launch can contribute, but measure different stages. These candidates provide distinct starting points.

Etzer Emile’s programme includes a 500-megawatt target. That is proposed capacity, not hours already delivered to every home. Transmission, distribution, price and maintenance connect it to household service. [1]

A 2014 account concerning Gandhy Dorfeuille describes a Saint-Louis du Sud electrification phase with a G24 million Treasury budget and G18 million available. The G6 million difference is not evidence of missing money. Launching does not establish every subsequent connection worked. [2]

Another site illustrates an energy-policy risk: BINUH reports equipment theft at Marchand-Dessalines in August 2026. That is not the 2014 project or an event attributed to either candidate. It shows why continuity requires more than construction. [3]

For Dorfeuille, connection and maintenance records would clarify experience. For Emile, initial service locations and costs would make the target concrete. The useful comparison is verifiable service, not subtraction between megawatts and gourdes.
