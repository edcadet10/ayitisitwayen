Wilson Jeudy in 2016: four million in cash, more than 18 million in payroll

A simple comparison explains the problem he described. It does not establish responsibility or trace every gourde.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Returning as Delmas mayor in June 2016, Wilson Jeudy described the administration as bankrupt. Radio Métropole reported his statement that only four million gourdes remained in the treasury against monthly payroll exceeding 18 million. He blamed interim administrators. That was Jeudy’s allegation, not a verified audit conclusion. [1]

If those figures were accurate, available cash covered less than one quarter of one month’s payroll. The gap exceeded 14 million gourdes before other expenses. That makes his reported cash-flow crisis concrete: funds on hand could not meet the immediate obligation. [1]

Jeudy also cited revenue exceeding 400 million gourdes for the preceding year. Comparing a year’s receipts with one day’s remaining balance does not prove the difference disappeared. Expenditure, debts, payment dates and other accounts must be reconciled. Otherwise a large number can accuse without explaining the accounts. [1]

He announced placing nearly all employees on standby while restructuring. Assessing his presidential credentials requires handover accounts and the restructuring’s results. Diagnosing financial distress is a first step; restoring public services requires its own evidence. [1]
