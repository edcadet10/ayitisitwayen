Salomon in 2020: a permanent Senate, but who chose its president?

An old crisis shows the difference between defending an institution and receiving authority to lead it.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On January 13, 2020, amid the dispute over parliamentary terms, Jean-Marie Junior Salomon announced that he was provisionally assuming the Senate presidency. Rezo Nòdwès reports that he invoked Constitution Article 95-1 on the Senate’s permanent character. [1]

The same article supplies another reference point. Senate secretary-general Rony Gilot recalled internal rules providing a secret-ballot election for the new bureau and an interim role for the oldest member pending that election. [1]

Those arguments address different questions. One concerns the institution’s continued existence; the other concerns how it grants someone authority to lead. Even if a crisis should not extinguish an institution, that does not automatically identify who can speak for it.

We do not use this news report to adjudicate every disputed term. It records positions expressed at the time; it does not replace the decisions or minutes that would show how the crisis was resolved. [1]

For a presidential candidate emphasizing institutions, this raises a practical question about power: when texts and political actors conflict, what decision process does he accept? The answer matters especially when that process might deny him what he wants.
