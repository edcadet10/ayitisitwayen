Jude Célestin: from boycott to a new election

Challenging an election, obtaining a rerun and winning it are different outcomes. The 2015–2016 crisis illustrates the distinction.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Jude Célestin placed second behind Jovenel Moïse in the October 2015 first-round results. The UN’s March 2016 report describes his campaign’s subsequent runoff boycott amid challenges to the process’s credibility. The January 24 runoff did not occur: on January 22, the CEP suspended operations, citing security concerns. [1]

Under provisional President Jocelerme Privert, a verification commission examined 3,235 of 13,725 polling-station result sheets. The August UN report says it found serious, systematic irregularities and recommended a rerun. That was not a recount declaring Célestin the winner. [2]

International observers differed over the remedy. The EU expert report recalls that its 2015 observation mission closed in June 2016 following disagreement over annulment. A separate expert mission followed the November 20 election, won by Jovenel Moïse in the first round. Célestin’s successful challenge to the earlier process did not become an electoral victory. [3]

Two questions should remain separate when assessing the candidate: what safeguards did he demand, and how effectively did he mobilize voters? A boycott can pressure a process without proving every accusation. A rerun can determine a winner without erasing the problems that undermined confidence in the earlier vote.
