Haiti’s registration growth is local: what Tardieu and Deronneth’s party entries show

The presidential count stays at 33 while entries for local posts account for almost all the net increase. Entries still need careful interpretation.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Between our November 15 and November 18 readings, the CEP registry rose from 11,142 to 12,090 entries: a net increase of 948. All 33 presidential source rows were unchanged. Most movement happened farther down the ballot. [1] [2]

Mayoral entries increased by 218, CASEC by 380 and ASEC by 338. Together, those local posts account for 936 of the 948 net increase, or 98.7%. Senate entries rose by three and deputy entries by nine. These are our calculations from the two dated snapshots. [1] [2]

The current list records 331 local-post entries under EAPH, Tardieu’s listed structure, and 46 under DVNH, Deronneth’s. We counted exact party labels across mayoral, CASEC and ASEC rows. We did not combine supposed aliases or assume coalitions. [1]

An entry is not a vote, an approved candidacy, a complete team or a separate locality. Several members of one local team can produce several rows. The figures measure the recorded footprint, not popularity, organisational quality or governing ability.

Deronneth has advocated better local pay; the 2018 commission chaired by Tardieu proposed reorganising local representation. Those dated positions differ from their current party-registration totals. Neither the totals nor a historical proposal establish what every listed member supports. [3] [4]

The next electoral questions belong in communes and sections too. Who is on the team, what work will each person do, and how will residents demand accounts? Presidential promises about decentralisation become clearer when voters can inspect the people and responsibilities underneath them.
