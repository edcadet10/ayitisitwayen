Deus Deronneth in 2024: challenging a council is not annulling it

He invoked sovereignty and separation of powers. Understanding the position requires distinguishing a candidate’s argument from a judicial decision.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On March 29, 2024, Rezo Nòdwès reported Deus Deronneth challenging the constitutional basis of the presidential council being prepared with CARICOM facilitation. He relied on articles 1 and 59: national sovereignty and the delegation of power to the three branches of the state. [1]

The article says Deronneth emphasized the judiciary’s duty to uphold constitutional principles during the crisis. That was an interpretation and political demand. It cites no judgment annulling the council or authorizing a judge to assume all executive authority. [1]

The April agreement subsequently described a different route: nine council members, seven voting and two observers. It records the negotiated arrangement. Signing an agreement does not itself adjudicate every constitutional objection Deronneth raised. [2]

Assessing the candidate means asking how his legal position would become a governed route out of crisis: which institution decides, and what limits apply to temporary authority? Respect for the Constitution is a principle. An implementable mechanism that avoids another unchecked authority must follow.
