Martelly’s Cap-Haïtien flight: what was new, and what came before

The airline’s own announcement identifies a particular route. It also reminds readers that the company had served Haiti for decades.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Martelly’s October 2, 2014 post places him aboard American Airlines’ first commercial service to Cap-Haïtien. The destination matters: the post does not claim Haiti was receiving its first commercial flight. [1]

American’s July announcement names Miami–Cap-Haïtien, with an October 2 start subject to government approval. It says the company had served Haiti for more than forty years and describes the northern route as additional to its longstanding Port-au-Prince service. [2]

Métropole’s report the following day describes the inaugural flight and Martelly’s participation. But it names Fort Lauderdale, while the airline’s release names Miami. That conflict is a reason to seek the operational record, not to merge the two endpoints. [3] [2]

The same newspaper account traces airport expansion to a project launched under René Préval and reports Martelly acknowledging earlier Venezuelan support. Those financing details remain attributed to the report; original agreements and project accounts were not recovered here. [3]

An inauguration photograph captures one moment in a longer chain: planning, financing, construction, approval and actual service. A fair account can recognise the new connection while asking how credit and responsibility were shared. Neither personal credit nor political blame should swallow the institutions that made the service possible.
