Muscadin: after arrest, what justice awaits?

Prison data put trials, care and detention inside the security debate. Arrest is not a verdict.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A security operation does not end when someone enters a police vehicle. If the state arrests a person, it must bring the case to justice and answer for detention conditions. Rule-bound authority is part of security.

Jean Ernest Muscadin brings a prosecutor’s record to the campaign. BINUH’s April–June 2026 report raises serious allegations about his use of that authority. Those allegations remain distinct from conviction and from the national prison population. [1]

The July–September report supplies context: 7,557 people imprisoned on September 30, 80% in pretrial detention, with 69 deaths during the quarter. This establishes neither everyone’s innocence nor everyone’s guilt; arrest does not supply a trial outcome. [2]

We do not attribute those prison deaths to the candidate. The data require answers from any security plan: case processing, defence access, care, water and detention conditions. More arrests require explaining these capacities too.

A community fearing gangs has a legitimate demand for protection. A system determining cases through evidence offers a more meaningful standard than arrest totals alone. The chain from complaint to judgment needs a plan.

Sources and verification limits
