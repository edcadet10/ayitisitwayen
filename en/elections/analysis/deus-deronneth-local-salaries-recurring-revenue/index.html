Deronneth’s local-pay proposal needs a monthly revenue story

Better pay deserves debate. Historical fund allegations and future payroll obligations require different accounts.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

In October 2024, Deronneth alleged that roughly 160 billion gourdes from the local-government development fund had been diverted over 28 years. In the same post, he advocated monthly pay above 70,000 gourdes for ASEC members and above 80,000, plus expenses, for CASEC members. [1]

The diversion claim remains an allegation in this review. Testing it requires annual receipts, the rules governing permitted spending, actual transfers and audits. A total asserted across 28 years is not already an established recoverable balance.

The salary proposal deserves its own calculation. At 70,000 a month, one position costs 840,000 gourdes over twelve months; at 80,000, it costs 960,000 before expenses. Because the proposal says more than those amounts, these are reference thresholds, not final annual salaries. The number of paid positions would determine the wider bill.

A recovery from the past and money arriving every month solve different problems. Even a successful recovery does not by itself identify next year’s recurring revenue. Deronneth should publish that revenue plan rather than leave readers to infer it from the historical allegation.

My test is straightforward: explain the work, count the positions, publish the annual cost and name a durable funding source. Add attendance, spending and service checks. Local officials should have the means to do their jobs; citizens should have the means to see what that pay delivers.
