Lucmanne Delille and G9: two dates his account must address

A UN report and a published account of his 2022 response place G9’s formation differently.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

June 10, 2020: the UN Secretary-General’s report dates the formation of the G9 an fanmi alliance. July 9, 2020: the same report dates Rockefeller Vincent’s replacement of Lucmanne Délille as justice minister. Those dates are 29 days apart. [1]

Yet in Gazette Haïti’s account of Delille’s April 4, 2022 response to Joseph Jouthe, Delille says G9 formed after his departure, rather than during his ministry. The outlet also reports his rejection of the former prime minister’s allegations and his own account of dismissal. [2]

The discrepancy is specific: in the UN chronology, the alliance existed before he left office. That calls for clarification of what “formation” meant in the 2022 appearance, or how the statement was reported. We reviewed the article, not the entire broadcast video. [1] [2]

This does not establish that Delille created, financed or supported G9. Nor does it settle why he was dismissed. A group’s emergence, a minister’s knowledge and the actions he took require different evidence.

To examine a candidate’s security record, start with a checkable timeline. Then seek orders, operational reports and decisions showing what authorities did. A political account told afterwards must withstand comparison with dates recorded at the time.
