Olivier Barrau: at 16, what protection would the youth corps provide?

The proposal includes minors. Schooling, work, consent and protection need rules, without conflating civic service with an armed force.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A 16-year-old may want a trade and a chance to serve the community while still needing child protection. A programme including adults should explain tasks, hours and supervision.

Juno7 reports Olivier Barrau proposing a three-year pilot youth corps for ages 16–35, covering community service, sport, civic education and vocational training. This is neither a verified armed programme nor a national service verified as operating. [1]

BINUH reports insufficient child-recruitment prevention in 2026 and describes a 2023 protocol for transfer, reception and care of children associated with gangs. This is separate from Barrau’s proposal; we attribute no gang recruitment to him. [2]

That context calls for precision rather than automatic suspicion. For minors, the plan should explain schooling, consent, tasks, transport and a safe complaint route. At every age, access should not depend on party membership.

These are proposed design criteria, not a court’s findings about the programme. A useful youth service would leave participants more capable and protected. That outcome would give its name practical meaning.
