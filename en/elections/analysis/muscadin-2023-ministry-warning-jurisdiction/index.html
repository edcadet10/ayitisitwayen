Muscadin and the 2023 reprimand: security and limits on power

An earlier ministry letter raises a question that goes beyond a prosecutor’s popularity.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In December 2023, the justice ministry reminded Jean Ernest Muscadin of accountability obligations and jurisdictional limits. In the letter reported by Juno7, the ministry criticized intervention in another jurisdiction without authorization and a roadblock supporters had mounted in his name. [1]

This is the ministry’s position in a reprimand, not a criminal conviction we present. The document matters because it shows his supervising authority questioning how he exercised power, beyond the outcomes he claimed. [1]

The article also discusses a separate letter to Ronald Richemond. Criticism of a hotel owner’s questioning concerns Richemond. It must not be transferred to Muscadin because both names appear in one article. [1]

For the public, the issue is who can impose limits when a State agent says action is necessary for security. If authorization, oversight and explanation apply only to unpopular officials, popularity replaces the rules.

Scrutinizing Muscadin’s candidacy therefore requires an answer about oversight he would accept over his own actions: who can review an order, what evidence is preserved and where a citizen can complain. Protecting residents and accounting for the means used are parts of the same public responsibility.
