Joanas Gué after the 2008 storms: losses are not recovery funding

Damage estimates, recovery plans and budget funding are separate stages. Combining them makes a public response harder to assess.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On September 18, 2008, Agriculture Minister Joanas Gué told Radio Métropole that storms had affected more than 63,000 hectares of cultivable land, with losses exceeding 180 million dollars. The same report quotes Kozepep’s Charles Suffrard saying he could not yet provide a comprehensive assessment for the Artibonite valley. [1]

Gué also cited 123 million dollars for river protection, inputs and canal repairs, and 200 million gourdes he hoped to obtain quickly from the budget. These are different currencies and different measures: estimated recovery work versus hoped-for budget resources. [1]

Subtracting 200 million gourdes from 123 million dollars cannot establish a financing gap. Even with conversion, the scope and date of each estimate matter. A lost harvest’s value need not equal the cost of restoring the production system.

Assessing Gué’s experience requires following the response from assessment through financing, disbursement and recovery. The report records announcements and uncertainty. It does not establish final spending or how many farmers resumed production. [1]
