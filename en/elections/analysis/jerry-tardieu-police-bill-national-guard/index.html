Jerry Tardieu: from a PNH bill to a national-guard proposal

The legislative record and new security proposal need to be read stage by stage.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In May 2016, Rezo Nòdwès reported deputy Jerry Tardieu submitting a 98-article bill to reorganize the PNH. Its account described new directorates for territorial policing, borders, training and prison administration, among other functions. [1]

On his 2026 campaign site, Tardieu proposes strengthening police and army and creating a youth national guard to recover territory controlled by armed groups. This is a campaign proposal, not a force we have verified as established. [2]

The first issue is command. Who recruits, trains, pays and gives orders? When someone carries weapons and public authority, accountability rules matter alongside equipment. The plan needs to explain how a new force relates to existing institutions.

The second issue is what followed the earlier bill. Submission, passage, promulgation and implementation are different stages. The 2016 article establishes submission; it cannot stand in for every later outcome. [1]

For the public, a new institution’s name does not guarantee a different quality of protection. Rules, oversight and the ability to sustain service will show what changes.
