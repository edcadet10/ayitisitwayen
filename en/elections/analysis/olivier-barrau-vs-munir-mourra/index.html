Olivier Barrau vs Munir Mourra: an agricultural bank needs an answer when a harvest fails

Credit, irrigation and insurance belong to the same farming season. They address different problems, but their rules need to work together.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

When a harvest fails, a new loan does not automatically erase old debt. That is why Munir Mourra’s agricultural-financing proposals deserve reading alongside the insurance experience of Olivier Barrau’s company, without treating the mechanisms as identical.

The MPH manifesto proposes an agricultural bank, irrigation-channel clearing and agricultural distribution centres through public-private partnerships. The party identifies Munir Joseph Mourra as the candidate carrying the manifesto. These are proposals on credit and production conditions, not a bank verified as funded or operating. [1] [2]

In 2022, AIC participated with WFP, WRMS and SCOR in an insurance pilot for 4,990 smallholders. Its report identifies Barrau as board president. WFP gives a ceiling of about US$900 per farmer divided between excess rainfall and rainfall deficit. That ceiling is not an amount every farmer received. [3] [4]

Credit pays for inputs before sale. Irrigation can reduce some production risks. Insurance can transfer financial risk when contract conditions are met. But a rainfall measure may not match each field’s loss; IFC identifies that limitation in index insurance. The three tools need connecting without promising to erase every loss. [5]

For Mourra, the bank needs rules on disaster repayment and politically connected lending. Assessing Barrau’s experience requires payouts and exclusions. The 2022 pilot is not, by itself, his campaign programme. Their common question is whether a bad season leaves farmers a route to restart, or simply another debt.
