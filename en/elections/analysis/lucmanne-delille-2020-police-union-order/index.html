Lucmanne Delille: a signature on police officers’ right to organize

The March 2020 order supplies a concrete measure in his ministerial record. Signing a text and securing its implementation are connected tasks.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A March 10, 2020 order by Joseph Jouthe’s government amended the status of PNH personnel. The text reproduced by HaïtiLibre bears the names of the prime minister and Justice Minister Lucmanne Delille. It provides for officers of all ranks to associate and organize in unions to defend their rights under conditions set by law. [1]

That is more specific than calling a minister simply “for” or “against” police. It shows Delille’s participation in a government decision recognizing organizational space. It does not establish sole authorship or that he independently created the entire public-service union framework.

A written right needs workable rules: representation, grievances and dispute resolution while public service continues. Recognizing organization does not endorse every act by any group claiming to speak for officers. The measure must be examined alongside its implementation.

The report presented the order pending a broader public-service association and union decree. This examines the 2020 step, not every current rule on strikes or police service. For Delille as a candidate, the record concerns how the decision changed relations between administration and personnel. [1]
