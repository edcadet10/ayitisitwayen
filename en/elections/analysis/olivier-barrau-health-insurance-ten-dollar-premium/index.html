Olivier Barrau and ten-dollar insurance: a premium is not the whole cost of care

AIC supplies a 2022 price indicator for part of its clientele. It guarantees neither free care nor a current price for every family.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A family paying insurance monthly still needs to know what happens when someone falls ill: the clinic payment, where its card is accepted and exclusions. An apparently affordable price should help answer those questions.

In its 2022 social indicators, AIC says 53% of insured people in its sickness/death portfolio paid monthly premiums no higher than US$10. That is the company’s statement for fiscal 2021–2022; its report identifies Barrau as board president. [1] [2]

It does not mean 53% of Haitians were insured. It does not say every product or family costs ten dollars or that the price still applies in November 2026. It describes a customer group and period, not a national presidential-programme price. [1]

Assessment needs premiums alongside coverage ceilings, customer contributions, exclusions and reimbursement delays. Two policies can share a premium but produce different results for the same illness. We do not pretend this report contains every individual policy term.

As a candidate, Barrau can explain the public-service lessons and remaining limits of insurance experience. For a family, success is more than an affordable monthly payment: it is obtaining care without expenses the policy failed to make clear.

Sources and verification limits

  1. AIC: social indicators on health/death premiums, pages 32–33

    Alternative Insurance Company

    Company statement for 2021–2022: 53% of insured people in its sickness/death portfolio pay at most US$10 monthly. Not every product, family or November 2026 price. The tables do not establish coverage of all care costs.

  2. AIC 2022: leadership, audit letter and financial results, pages 10, 26 and 28

    Alternative Insurance Company

    The table is in thousands of gourdes. Percentage changes are calculated from printed amounts; these are company accounts, not a candidate’s personal assets.
