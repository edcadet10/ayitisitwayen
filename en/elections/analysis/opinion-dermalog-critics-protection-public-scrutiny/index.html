Opinion: scrutiny of a contract needs people able to question it without threats

Dermalog has another documentary trail: protection of those who criticized it. That does not permit inventing the threats’ authors.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

When a state contract is contested, the public needs more than competing media accounts. People must be able to complain, provide information and answer justice without fearing for their lives. That protection serves complainants and accused people alike by allowing evidence to be examined.

In their February 5, 2021 communication, UN experts relayed threats against Camille Occius after his Dermalog criticism and actions. They requested state information on investigation and protection. The letter describes unidentified perpetrators. We attribute the threats to neither Martine Moïse, the company nor another candidate. [1]

There is also a specific decision: IACHR resolution 74/2021, dated September 4, 2021, requests Haiti to protect Occius, enable his work and report on investigation. The Commission considered the risk serious and urgent on its initial assessment. It specifies that the measure does not prejudge rights violations or individual guilt. [2]

We lack a complete record of later implementation. We also retain Martine’s office’s denial of influence over the contract. Her right to defend herself and a critic’s right to remain safe are compatible. Both require a functioning documentary process. [3]

Our view is that candidates should protect those examining contracts even when criticism is inconvenient. That requires independent complaint channels and verifiable follow-up. Otherwise the public may never learn whether a contract was sound; it may learn only who was most afraid to speak.

Sources and verification limits

  1. UN experts request answers about threats against rights defenders

    UN Special Procedures mandate holders · 2021-02-05

    Communication AL HTI 1/2021, especially pages 3 and 5–6. It relays threat allegations and seeks investigation; it identifies neither Martine Moïse nor Dermalog as the threat authors and is not a verdict.

  2. IACHR: resolution 74/2021 on Camille Occius’s protection

    Inter-American Commission on Human Rights · 2021-09-04

    Pages 9–10 request protection, consultation and follow-up. This precautionary risk assessment does not adjudicate a candidate’s guilt. State nonresponse is reported as of the resolution, not all later years. Private family details are not reproduced.

  3. Martine Moïse’s office denied signing or influencing the Dermalog contract

    HaitiLibre / Bureau de la Première dame · 2018-12-10

    HaitiLibre reproduces the office’s response. It is her position at the time, not a judicial finding or a response newly obtained by us.
