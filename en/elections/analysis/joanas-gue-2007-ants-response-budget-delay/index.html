Joanas Gué: what a 2007 ant campaign reveals about agricultural response

The problem had been assessed; resources and action were still being arranged. The gap between those stages is where management can be examined.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In September 2007, as ants damaged communities in Limbé, Plaisance and Pilate, Joanas Gué was secretary of state for agriculture. Radio Métropole reported his announcement of a three-year campaign. He estimated needs at 100 million gourdes and said 50 million had been allocated in the 2007–2008 budget for its first phase. [1]

The revealing detail was timing. Gué said ministry specialists had assessed the infestation a year earlier and produced a document. He acknowledged that delay had worsened the situation. Plaisance’s mayor had made another urgent appeal for intervention two weeks before the report. [1]

For a household living from crops and livestock, assessment is not yet relief. An administration must turn diagnosis into decisions, release resources and check whether intervention works. This episode shows why ministry experience should be examined through response times as well as announced projects.

The 2007 report supplies neither final expenditure accounts nor measured campaign outcomes. It does not establish that the 50 million was spent or went missing. Completing Gué’s record on this episode requires intervention reports, disbursement dates and evidence of what changed in the affected areas.
