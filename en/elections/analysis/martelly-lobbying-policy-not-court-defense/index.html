What Martelly’s lobbying contract asks Washington to change

The document targets sanctions policy. It is neither a verdict nor a promise of success.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

The agreement in FARA filing 7793 calls for advice and advocacy aimed at the U.S. Treasury and State Department concerning policy that uses OFAC to sanction foreign individuals. This gives the work a specific public-policy target. [1]

Invisible Investigations is the contracting client; Michel Joseph Martelly is the named beneficiary. The letter frames the work as seeking review of policies or designations considered insufficiently grounded in facts or law. That is the client’s position, not a finding that the U.S. government agrees. [1]

Services include developing strategies, communicating with officials and reporting progress. The agreement guarantees neither a meeting, a policy change nor removal of sanctions. It permits suspension of work that raises legal concerns. [1]

Another express limit: services exclude litigation, criminal matters and congressional investigations unless separately agreed in writing. This lobbying filing therefore does not establish that the firm represents Martelly in court proceedings concerning Jovenel Moïse’s assassination. [1]

Assessing results requires later disclosures of contacts or activities and any official OFAC decision. A contract alone cannot establish that a U.S. official agreed to a meeting or changed position.
