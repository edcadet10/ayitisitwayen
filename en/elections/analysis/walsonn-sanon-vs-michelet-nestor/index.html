Walsonn Sanon vs Michelet Nestor: whose consent counts?

Rejecting an agreement and winning a nomination measure different things. Both invite clear rules on who participated.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A room can announce consensus while others remain outside its door. In politics, the word alone is insufficient: who could speak, who agreed and how was disagreement handled?

Scoop FM reported on December 27, 2022 that SOLID leader Walsonn Sanon rejected the December 21 agreement. He said promised revisions addressing his organization’s reservations had not preceded the announced signing. His criticism concerned process and participation, not rejection of every agreement in principle. [1]

Kayimit News reports Michelet Nestor registering for DPA in November 2026 after selection by consensus. That records an organizational nomination, not nationwide support. Likewise, refusing the 2022 agreement is not an electoral result for Sanon. [2]

In 2025 Sanon called for renewal rooted in local life. That invites examination of his organization’s own leadership selection and ways members can challenge decisions. For DPA, the equivalent question concerns participation in its nomination consensus. [3]

We lack complete internal minutes to assign democracy scores. What can be said is that a method gains credibility when those who lose can understand its rules too. Consent needs more than the winner’s announcement.
