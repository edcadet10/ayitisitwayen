Gary Bodeau vs Jocelerme Privert: a vote count is not the entire story of selecting a leader

A 2016 National Assembly vote and U.S. allegations concerning 2018–2019 appointments are different records of parliamentary power.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

When Parliament selects a leader or approves an appointment, the public needs more than raised hands. Which rules give the vote meaning and which interests surround it? Jocelerme Privert and Gary Bodeau present different evidentiary records.

The UN’s 2016 transition report records 77 National Assembly votes for Privert against Edgard Leblanc’s 34. It installed a provisional president under a time-limited agreement. That is neither a direct popular election nor validation of all later conduct. [1]

For Bodeau, the U.S. Treasury in 2023 described alleged payments for appointment votes in 2018 and 2019. It cited 500–600 million gourdes businessmen supplied to Chamber members in the 2019 episode. That does not assign the whole amount to Bodeau’s pocket. He denied allegations behind the sanction. [2] [3]

Those payment allegations are not transferred to Privert’s 2016 election; the sources establish no such link. Nor is Bodeau’s administrative sanction turned into a criminal conviction. An official count and allegations about another vote’s mechanism require different records.

Both can commit to records of rules, appointee files, interests and votes in public selections. That lets citizens distinguish a majority from unlimited authority and investigate money without imagining it behind every decision.

Sources and verification limits

  1. UN S/2016/225: electoral disputes and Privert’s installation

    United Nations Secretary-General · 2016-03-08

    Paragraphs 6–13 describe the electoral sequence, February agreement and National Assembly vote. A contemporary report, not a criminal judgment.

  2. Gary Bodeau sanctions — allegations of payments for appointment votes

    U.S. Department of the Treasury · 2023-04-05

    Alleges corruption and influence over votes. General statements about gangs must not be recast as a criminal verdict against him.

  3. Bodeau’s response after the U.S. sanctions

    Gazette Haïti · 2023-04-06

    Reports his denial and intention to use legal mechanisms. It does not establish which later challenges were filed or their outcomes.
