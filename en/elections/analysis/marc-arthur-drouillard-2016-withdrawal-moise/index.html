Marc Arthur Drouillard: what a 2016 footnote adds to the small vote total

An old result does not explain an alliance. The observers’ report adds another piece.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In the provisional-results annex of the EU report on the 2016 election, Marc-Arthur Drouillard has 978 votes. Earlier in the same report, a footnote says he withdrew in favour of Jovenel Moïse on September 27, 2016. [1]

Both facts can coexist. An announced withdrawal does not automatically remove a name from every electoral material or prevent votes for it. We do not use that provisional total as a measure of Drouillard’s support in 2026.

The useful issue is the political choice: why did he support Moïse then, what aims did he state, and how does he assess that decision now? A past alliance merits explanation; it does not establish a present alliance.

Another limit matters. Supporting a candidate in an election does not establish participation in others’ later conduct. Each allegation about an act requires its own evidence. A political relationship has a date and a specific meaning.

When tracing a career, the question extends beyond “how many votes?” to “whom did he help into power, why, and what did he learn?” The footnote makes the record more informative than a ranking alone.
