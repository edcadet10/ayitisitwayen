Kely Bastien vs Jocelerme Privert: when provisional power reaches its deadline

A 2010 position and a 120-day mandate in 2016 raise a shared question: who decides after the deadline?

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A crisis can make an election difficult. That does not itself establish who governs during delay or for how long. Kely Bastien discussed that dilemma in 2010; Jocelerme Privert led a transition confronting it in 2016.

After the 2010 earthquake, Senate president Bastien urged examination of the option closest to the Constitution. Métropole reports his emphasis on election efforts within 90 days and criticism of a one-year mandate for a government lacking popular legitimacy. That is his position, not an enacted calendar attributed to him. [1]

In 2016, the National Assembly elected Privert provisional president under an agreement allowing no more than 120 days. The UN’s August report says the term expired June 14 while the Assembly failed to vote on continued executive-governance arrangements. That is not a court ruling validating an extension. [2] [3]

The crises were distinct. Government formation, vote verification and CEP decisions contributed to 2016 delay; these are not all assigned to one person. A deadline nevertheless needs a rule for what happens when it is missed. [3]

Bastien can explain mechanisms making his 2010 principle workable. Privert can explain the basis presented for continuing and scrutiny accepted. For both, a transition promise needs a public exit beyond the discretion of whoever holds office.
