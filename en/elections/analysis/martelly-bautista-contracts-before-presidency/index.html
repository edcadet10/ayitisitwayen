Martelly and the Bautista contracts: the story began before his term

Contract dates and later decisions matter. They allow responsibility to be traced without assigning every transaction to the same president.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

According to the Senate commission’s 2016 PetroCaribe report, six major contracts with companies linked to Félix Bautista were signed on November 8, 2010. They covered Parliament and housing at Bowenfield and Fort National, worth about US$348 million. That is a contract value, not an established sum of missing money. [1]

Michel Martelly took office on May 14, 2011, after those initial contracts. The report also describes decisions to retain or modify contracts after the change of government. Inheriting a contract and deciding to continue it are different moments in the same chain of responsibility. [1]

In 2018, the US Treasury sanctioned Bautista and five companies, including Hadom and Constructora Rofi. It alleged reconstruction-related corruption and cited a company receiving more than US$10 million for unfinished work. The release does not identify a Haitian president receiving that money. [2]

Bautista disputed corruption allegations in litigation discussed by a US court in 2021. The opinion we reviewed dismissed four claims concerning State Department entry restrictions; it was not a criminal judgment about Haiti’s contracts. His name remains in the OFAC sanctions snapshot checked November 15, 2026. [3] [4]

Assessing Martelly’s record means identifying who authorized each continuation, change and payment during his term. Original contracts, later signed amendments and completion reports must be read together. A company name or sanctions designation cannot replace that chain of evidence.

Sources and verification limits

  1. Senate commission’s 2016 PetroCaribe report

    Senate of Haiti / public report copy

    Public 98-page copy. For CNE, PDF pages 34–37 cover the budget, purchase table, testimony and inventory limitation. A parliamentary inquiry recommending further investigation, not a conviction.

  2. US sanctions against Félix Ramón Bautista Rosario and five companies

    U.S. Department of the Treasury · 2018-06-12

    The administrative release cites Haitian reconstruction and unfinished work. It does not name a Haitian official receiving a payment in the operation it describes.

  3. Bautista-Rosario: decision on four State Department claims

    U.S. District Court, District of Columbia · 2021-09-22

    Case 1:20-cv-2782, document 18, 11 pages. Dismisses four State Department claims without adjudicating a criminal allegation about Haitian contracts. Also records Bautista’s denial of corruption allegations.

  4. SDN list: Félix Ramón Bautista Rosario entry checked November 15, 2026

    Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)

    Listed as ROSARIO, Felix Ramon Bautista, under GLOMAG. An administrative sanctions list, not a criminal-conviction register.
