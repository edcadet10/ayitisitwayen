Jude Célestin and CNE: the machines need an inventory, not only a budget

A Senate report separates the allocation, the contract record and the equipment investigators could verify.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

The Senate commission’s 2016 PetroCaribe report revisits heavy-equipment purchases for the National Equipment Center, CNE. It identifies Jude Célestin as the institution’s head during the period and recalls parliamentary testimony about the purchases. The equipment allocation was US$80 million. [1]

One contract-table entry distinguishes orders from deliveries: 81 trucks instead of 100, with US$2,565,000 deducted from the total. Calling those 19 trucks “missing” would ignore the adjustment the table itself records for the undelivered quantity. [1]

The commission raised a different, substantial problem: it had not obtained an exhaustive CNE inventory enabling it to assess all the equipment and its existence. A purchase record alone cannot establish where a machine is, whether it works or what service it provides. [1]

This is a parliamentary inquiry, not a criminal conviction of Célestin. It does not allow us to put US$80 million into one person’s pocket with a sentence. Several institutions were involved in financing and procurement; each actor’s responsibility needs tracing through their signatures and decisions. [1]

A useful record for a candidate with this experience would connect three files: contracts, payments and an inventory with machine identifiers. Which equipment served the public, which broke down and who maintained it? That is where public spending becomes a question about the service citizens received.
