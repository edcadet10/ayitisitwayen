René St-Fort: displacement should not erase a vote

His registration position highlights an easily missed barrier: an office can remain open while the citizen cannot reach it.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On March 16, 2026, René St-Fort told Vant Bèf Info he supported CEP outreach to voters. The PRN leader emphasized registration barriers for people displaced by violence and called for attention to remote areas. [1]

The point extends beyond distance. A procedure may formally exist while remaining inaccessible in practice. For a displaced household, knowing where to go and being able to travel safely can matter as much as office hours. This is an access analysis, not the account of a family interviewed by us.

That suggests a concrete test: does a plan reach displaced people, or mainly count those already able to travel independently? A registration total cannot answer without showing which groups and locations the service reaches.

St-Fort’s statement is not a costed operational plan. It establishes a priority he defended. Voters can ask how his own party helps people understand the process while keeping civic assistance independent of any obligation to support the party. [1]
