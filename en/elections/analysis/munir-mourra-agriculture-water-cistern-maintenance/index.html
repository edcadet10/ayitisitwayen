Munir Mourra: before a new centre, where is the water?

A family-cistern project shows why even small infrastructure needs maintenance, sharing rules and service checks.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An agricultural centre can have an impressive building while nearby fields lack water. In Munir Mourra’s MPH programme, centres and canal works deserve examination alongside needs closest to farmers’ homes. [1]

Another programme proposes a smaller intervention. PARSA’s June 2026 paper describes 95 additional family cisterns, each holding just under 20 cubic metres and serving five neighbouring households. These are proposed activities, not cisterns we verified as completed. [2]

Twenty cubic metres equals 20,000 litres; the described capacity is slightly lower. Tank capacity is not daily water availability. Rainfall, catchment roofs, maintenance and sharing change the service. This document is not a drinking-water quality test.

We do not attribute PARSA’s cistern proposal or implementation to the candidate. It provides a concrete example for assessing his agricultural promises: whose water problem an investment solves, and for how long.

Centres, canals and cisterns are not automatic substitutes. A useful plan would show where each belongs, who maintains it and how communities get repairs. Water service matters to fields more than buildings on a campaign poster.
