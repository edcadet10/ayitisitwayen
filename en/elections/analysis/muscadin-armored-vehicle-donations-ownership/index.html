Muscadin: who owns the armored vehicle donors bought?

After public fundraising and his resignation, an ownership and accountability question remains.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In March 2023, journalist Theriel Thelus launched fundraising for two armored vehicles for Jean Ernest Muscadin. Three months later, Juno7 reported delivery of one armored Nissan Patrol. Thelus stated that the vehicle cost $45,000 and armor $55,000: $100,000 together. [1] [2]

The fundraising target must remain separate from the reported delivery. Announcing two vehicles does not establish two were delivered. Thelus’s stated cost is not an invoice we verified, and the article is not an audited account of all receipts and expenditure. [1] [2]

The question becomes more concrete following Muscadin’s resignation. Juno7’s account of the ministry’s letter says minister Patrick Pélissier accepted his departure in November 2026. A vehicle serving a public function needs clear status when its recipient leaves office. [3]

Is the title held by the State, an organization or an individual? Who pays maintenance, insurance and fuel? Does an inventory or agreement govern use after departure? These are ordinary questions about an asset financed through a public appeal.

We do not present the donation as proof of corruption or automatic financing of the 2026 campaign. It shows how citizens’ support for security can create another obligation: knowing what they funded, who is accountable for it and what becomes of it.
