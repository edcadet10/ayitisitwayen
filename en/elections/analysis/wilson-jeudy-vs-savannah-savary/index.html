Wilson Jeudy vs Savannah Savary: employee numbers do not establish capacity to pay wages

A mayor’s statement on municipal cash and an entrepreneur’s employment account require separating available funds, costs and job totals.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Creating work and sustaining payroll belong together. Wilson Jeudy and Savannah Savary provide different kinds of statements to examine. We do not rank a municipality against a private company by employee totals.

Returning as Delmas mayor in June 2016, Jeudy said the treasury held four million gourdes against monthly payroll exceeding 18 million. He blamed interim administrators. Métropole reports his statement; it is not our verified audit of the cause. [1]

In her October 2026 Haitian Times interview, Savary says inherited businesses provide more than 100 jobs. She limited financial details for security reasons. That supplies neither a payroll amount nor business cash accounts. We invent none for comparison with the municipality. [2]

Cash held on one day and one month’s expenditure cover different periods. Assessment needs incoming revenue, debt and payment dates. Likewise, employment totals do not reveal wages, conditions or job stability. Each figure needs its own context.

Management evidence connects work performed to the means of paying for it. Jeudy can show municipal revenue and payroll records for his administration; Savary can explain business-interest information publishable without unnecessary private risk. A record needs more than a large job total.
