Kely Bastien: the transparency test predates this campaign

An old official briefing points to the public record behind the new candidacy.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Kely Bastien appears in international public records long before DELIVRANS selected him. A briefing in a 2011 trip report hosted by the U.S. Senate Foreign Relations Committee identifies the northern senator and records his election as Senate president in 2008. [1]

The briefing describes Senate transparency and better relations between branches as his objectives. It is not an audit establishing those objectives were achieved. Nor does it prove every meeting on the visit’s agenda occurred. [1]

That is where a former leader’s experience becomes a record for public examination. Assembly responsibilities point readers towards budgets, expenditure reports and decision records. A title identifies where to investigate; it does not predetermine the findings.

For a taxpaying household, transparency means something concrete: seeing where money goes and comparing it with services received. The same test applies to candidates coming from the Senate, a ministry or a company.

DELIVRANS’s November 2026 selection is a new political step. It neither erases earlier responsibilities nor proves wrongdoing. It makes the documents about those responsibilities relevant to readers again. [2]
