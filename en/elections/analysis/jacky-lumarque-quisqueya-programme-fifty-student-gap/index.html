Lumarque, Quisqueya and the fifty learners between two totals

An encouraging student story leads to a simple arithmetic check—and a larger question about measuring results.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In April 2026, Lumarque’s account celebrated a student’s path from disrupted schooling in La Saline to a law degree at Quisqueya. The university’s website describes a remedial programme and gives a numerical example from 2020–2021. [1] [2]

That webpage states a total of 5,277 learners: 2,720 preparing for the baccalaureate and 2,507 in ninth year. It is an institutional account written by the rector’s chief of staff, not an independent outcome audit. [2]

The two groups add up to 5,227. Fifty learners separate the stated breakdown from the stated total. A typing or aggregation error is plausible. This does not establish invented students or dishonesty. Lumarque’s post did not quote the disputed total. [2] [1]

The practical question is which total should anchor the programme’s results. How many entered, completed the course and passed? What did it cost? Which selection rules applied? Anonymous cohort figures can answer those questions without exposing students’ private records.

One person’s progress deserves to be understood on its own terms. Before an institutional example becomes an argument for national policy, readers need figures they can reproduce and results someone outside the institution has checked.
