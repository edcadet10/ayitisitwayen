Opinion: protecting a power plant belongs in the electricity promise

Building, connecting and protecting are three tasks. Energy projects should explain continuing service after launch.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A good solar panel cannot supply a household if essential system equipment is stolen. We believe electricity promises should explain protection, repairs and service responsibility alongside capacity.

BINUH reports Gran Grif looting the Marchand-Dessalines power plant on August 15, 2026, taking batteries, solar panels and other essential equipment. It provides a specific example of violence striking a service, not only people in the street. [1]

Munir Mourra’s MPH manifesto proposes energy investment. Gandhy Dorfeuille’s record includes a 2014 Saint-Louis du Sud electrification launch. Neither is the Marchand-Dessalines plant; we attribute neither its management nor the attack to them. [2] [3]

Future plans can learn from the danger. We would ask for risk planning, maintenance funds, spare parts and an outage-reporting route. Sensitive security details need protection while the public can still identify responsibility for continuity.

Our view: another launch need not be bad, but launch totals are not the most useful measure. When equipment fails or disappears, households need to know when power returns and who has the duty to restore it.

Sources and verification limits
