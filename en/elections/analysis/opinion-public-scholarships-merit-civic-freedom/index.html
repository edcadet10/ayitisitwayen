An institution-funded scholarship should remain a civic opportunity

Our view: crediting someone who opens a door should never create a political obligation for the recipient.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

Tardieu’s own 2016 scholarship account names BRH as financier of the first cohort and records partners’ promise of merit-based selection. It supplies a useful starting point: the person assembling partners and the institution providing funding should remain distinguishable. [1]

A deputy’s service can be credited without making an institutional opportunity a personal debt. A student should be free to express thanks, retain independent opinions and vote freely. Gratitude must not become an unwritten political contract.

Rules should precede the beneficiary list: eligibility, selection criteria, decision-makers and a way to challenge error. Publishing them protects both students and those who advocated the programme against unsupported allegations.

This does not allege vote-buying or partisan scholarship selection; the source establishes neither. Our principle applies to every candidate and programme: services should continue independently of personal loyalty. Leadership can leave its strongest legacy in an opportunity that remains open after power changes hands.
