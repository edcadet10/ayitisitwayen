Moïse Durocher: one and a half million jobs need more than a large number

The goal could change many families’ lives. Assessing it requires knowing which jobs, over what period and with what funding.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A profile published November 12, 2026 gives Moïse Durocher’s goal of 1,500,000 direct jobs. It connects production and training in marine science, animal production and the study of water and ecosystems. That is the program as presented in the article, rather than jobs already created. [1]

A little arithmetic clarifies the scale. If that goal covered five years, it would mean an average of 300,000 new jobs a year, or 25,000 a month. Five years is our illustration for the calculation; the article does not supply that deadline for the goal.

What would count as a new job? Seasonal work can be valuable without offering the security of year-round pay. Registering an existing job is different from creating a position. Without those definitions, two teams could count the same program differently.

The profile contains another number: Durocher rates his own satisfaction with his previous roles at 95 percent. That is his assessment, rather than 95 percent of voters or survey respondents. Those are different kinds of measurement. [1]

A trackable employment plan needs a clear first stage: how many positions, which employers, how much training and who pays? Once that stage is specified, voters can start testing the promise. Until then, they know the scale of the ambition without a way to verify delivery.
