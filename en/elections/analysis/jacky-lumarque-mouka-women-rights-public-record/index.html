Jacky Lumarque and Mouka: a public resource behind the speech

A 2023 address on women’s rights points to a verifiable institutional action. It offers a way to examine the record of a rector seeking office.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On March 9, 2023, Jacky Lumarque discussed women carrying family burdens amid forced displacement. In that address, the Université Quisqueya rector presented Mouka as an online documentary resource on gender and Haitian women’s rights, with research intended to be freely accessible. [1]

At least part of that announcement is verifiable. Mouka.ht is accessible and presents research records, publications and thematic resources on women’s rights. This is a more specific institutional output than a general declaration of commitment. It does not mean Lumarque produced it alone; the resource represents collective work. [2]

Existence and impact are different measures. A library can be valuable before its use by teachers, students or organizations has been quantified. Measuring impact requires usage evidence and examples connecting research to changes in training, services or decisions.

For voters, the episode suggests a way to examine experience: identify resources a candidate helped an institution create, credit other contributors and investigate results. A useful record extends beyond qualifications to what remains available for others after a speech ends.
