Jerry Tardieu and the diaspora: voting, representation and office differ

An earlier proposal helps separate questions about participation from abroad.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

At a 2016 gathering in Florida, Jerry Tardieu advocated diaspora representation in Haiti’s Parliament. Rezo Nòdwès reported that he wanted the idea included in a constitutional-amendment proposal. This documents a political project, not proof an operative right was created. [1]

His campaign site still includes diaspora rights among the legislative work he presents. That lets readers trace a theme over time, while requiring examination of the legal documents that followed the statements. [2]

At least three questions differ: can a person vote where they live, elect a representative for an overseas community, and run for an office in Haiti? A change to one does not automatically resolve the others.

For each promise, readers need the text, effective date and implementing institution. When constitutional change is required, the candidate should explain the legal route. A campaign statement cannot replace those steps.

This does not decide an individual voter’s legal position. It helps prevent the word “integration” from merging distinct rights. Current procedures require the latest CEP instructions and operative legal texts.
