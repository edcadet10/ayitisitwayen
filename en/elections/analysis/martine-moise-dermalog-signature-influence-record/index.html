Martine Moïse and Dermalog: a signature and influence are different questions

An identity-card contract, influence allegations and an explicit denial: following the decisions means keeping a complaint distinct from a conviction.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An identification card lets a citizen establish who they are. When the state changes the system, the question extends beyond the card-maker’s name: who chose it, on what grounds and who must account for the decision? Those questions make Dermalog relevant to scrutiny of Martine Moïse’s candidacy.

Their August 7, 2019 report describes a US$27.7 million contract. CONHANE, RNDDH, CE-JILAP and CARDH report unfavorable Court of Accounts opinions in February and April 2018. They also record the government’s position: requesting the court’s opinion was mandatory, following it was not. According to the report, the cabinet authorized ONI’s director to sign in April 2018. [1]

The report identifies ministers and ONI’s director as state representatives in the contract; it does not identify Martine as a signatory. It relays allegations about her influence in the process. These are different questions. An absent signature does not settle influence; an influence allegation does not establish that she decided or received money. [1]

Her response belongs in the same story. In the denial HaitiLibre published December 10, 2018, her office denied that she signed or used her influence to secure the contract. It said official travel could bring encounters with other Haitian delegations there for different reasons. An encounter is not proof of an arrangement. [2]

Juno7 reported 14 citizens filing a complaint against her and other officials on August 19, 2019. A complaint asks justice to examine allegations; it is not a verdict. The material reviewed yielded no final decision in this matter. Nor did we obtain the original signed contract, court opinions or complete court file. [3]

The story advances through records linking people to decisions: supplier-selection correspondence, authorizations, signatures and payments. The question for Martine is her precise role, if any. For other officials, it is the basis on which they committed the state. Scrutiny needs to reach those decisions, beyond a family name and political noise.
