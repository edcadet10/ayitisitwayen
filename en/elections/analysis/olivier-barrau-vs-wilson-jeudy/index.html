Olivier Barrau vs Wilson Jeudy: what kind of money does the figure measure?

A company balance sheet and a municipal cash statement are not two measures of the candidates’ personal wealth.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A large figure misleads without a definition. A company balance sheet reports assets and obligations at a date; a municipality’s cash statement reports money available at a moment. Neither automatically describes its leader’s personal money.

AIC’s 2022 balance sheet reports approximately G4.206 billion in assets and G2.755 billion in liabilities. This concerns the company Olivier Barrau led, not proof that all those assets are his or his current ownership share. [1]

In May 2016 Wilson Jeudy said Delmas municipality had G4 million in cash against monthly payroll exceeding G18 million. His statement describes liquidity pressure, not an independent audit establishing all assets, debts or causes. [2]

We do not subtract these figures to rank the candidates. Years, institutions and measures differ. They open a shared presidential question: can a leader explain available cash, controlled assets and obligations falling due?

For workers, cash on payday matters even when an institution owns substantial assets. For citizens, ownership matters even when cash is low. Good accounts make both visible; one impressive number cannot replace them.

Sources and verification limits

  1. AIC: 2022 balance sheet, page 29, in thousands of gourdes

    Alternative Insurance Company

    The company balance sheet separates assets, liabilities and all shareholders’ equity; audit letter on page 26. This is not the candidate’s personal asset declaration or a complete current shareholder register. Units retained.

  2. Wilson Jeudy describes Delmas’s finances on his 2016 return

    Amounts and mismanagement allegations are the mayor’s statements. This is neither an independent audit nor a judicial bankruptcy determination.
