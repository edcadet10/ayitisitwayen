René St-Fort and 30,000 supporters: a citizen’s name is not a blank signature

PRN’s challenge opens a debate about consent. Enabling verification need not expose everyone’s political choices.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

On September 16, 2026, Vant Bèf Info reported that PRN, through lawyers René St-Fort and Roger Balthazar, sought suspension or cancellation of the electoral process. It challenged the 30,000-member, adherent or supporter condition and requested publication of lists so citizens could check for use of their names without consent. [1]

A formal demand is not a judgment. It does not prove that every list is false or every person mentioned suffered fraud. Consent nevertheless deserves an answer distinct from the threshold dispute: can a citizen discover and challenge a party’s claim to their support? [1]

Our view is that verification should serve citizens without forcing everyone’s political affiliation into public view. People should be able to check their own entry, receive an answer and correct unauthorized enrolment. Parties should also retain evidence of how consent was obtained.

For St-Fort as a candidate, the test is applying the same principle within his own party. A total is insufficient without explaining how people agreed to be counted. Transparency should let citizens challenge false political affiliation without creating a new danger for them.
