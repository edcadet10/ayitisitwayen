Olivier Barrau: AIC’s business grew while profit fell

Two figures in one report tell different stories. Reading the table carefully changes how the candidate’s business record should be assessed.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

AIC’s 2022 report names Olivier Barrau as board president. Written premiums increased from approximately 1.507 billion gourdes in 2021 to 1.827 billion in 2022, a rise of about 21.2%. Yet net profit fell from about 167.6 million gourdes to 148.5 million, a decline of approximately 11.4%. [1]

The original table uses thousands of gourdes: “148,512” means 148,512,000 gourdes. Written premiums measure insurance business written, not profit remaining after expenses. The report attributes the profit decline to increased losses and includes Joseph Paillant’s audit opinion on the financial statements. [1]

Think of a shop that sells more while its costs also rise. Activity can expand even as the amount left at year-end shrinks. These figures prevent one growth percentage from standing in for the entire management record. Alone, they do not establish good or bad management.

These are one year’s company results, not Barrau’s salary, family wealth or campaign funds. Evaluating his experience as a candidate would require several years of results, customer-service evidence and a clear account of his responsibilities alongside those of other executives.

Sources and verification limits

  1. AIC 2022: leadership, audit letter and financial results, pages 10, 26 and 28

    Alternative Insurance Company

    The table is in thousands of gourdes. Percentage changes are calculated from printed amounts; these are company accounts, not a candidate’s personal assets.
