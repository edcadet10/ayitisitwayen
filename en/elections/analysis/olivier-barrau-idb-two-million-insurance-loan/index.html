Olivier Barrau and AIC: the IDB’s $2 million was not a campaign gift

The 2012 loan record identifies a specific financial relationship and purpose to examine.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In the IDB register, project HA-L1072 is a US$2 million loan to Alternative Insurance Company. Approved March 8, 2012, it formed part of an US$8 million operation. The register classifies it as non-sovereign-guaranteed private-sector financing. [1]

An IDB description says recapitalization was intended to help AIC expand insurance for lower-income people after the 2010 earthquake. The company’s annual report identifies Olivier Barrau as its chief executive in 2022. [2] [3]

Three things should stay separate: an institution’s loan to a company, a candidate’s personal assets and campaign money. This record documents the first. It does not turn the funds into a personal gift or election contribution.

A worthwhile question remains: did the operation’s social purpose reach its intended customers? Answering requires comparing coverage, premiums and claims paid. A purpose statement cannot measure those outcomes.

This is more useful than treating every international-bank relationship as suspicious. When a candidate presents business experience as a qualification, the public can examine whom that activity served and what it delivered.
