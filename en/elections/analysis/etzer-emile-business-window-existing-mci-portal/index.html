Etzer Emile and business creation in 48 hours: what is new about one window?

An MCI portal already exists online. The campaign promise needs to distinguish completing a form from obtaining every document a business needs.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A trader can complete an online form and still need another office to accept the paperwork. That distinction matters in Etzer Emile’s promise of faster business creation. The question extends beyond having a website to completing the process.

His programme announces a single window within the first 100 days, combining tax number, business license and registration. It sets five-day creation in 2029 and 48 hours in 2033. Those are separate stages; 48 hours is not the first-100-days deadline. [1]

MCI already presents an official portal for individual-business registration, application tracking and certificate checks. In January 2026, the ministry held a workshop with banks and cooperatives on its digital documents’ validity. That documents a service and recognition effort, not complete integration across government. [2] [3]

Emile’s promise is therefore neither automatically redundant nor already achieved. It may cover functions the present portal does not. The campaign needs to identify services retained, offices connected and steps still requiring travel.

A practical test follows an application from submission to documents accepted by banks and government. Publish total time, cost and refusal reasons. That lets an entrepreneur see whether a new portal name brings a new service.
