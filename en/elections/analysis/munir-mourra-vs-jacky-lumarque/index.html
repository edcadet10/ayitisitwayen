Munir Mourra vs Jacky Lumarque: a campus needs a budget after construction

A proposed campus for at least 4,000 students and a rector’s experience raise questions about fees, teachers and the university’s purpose.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A campus can have buildings without enough teachers or working laboratories. Constructing a site differs from financing a university over years. That provides a way to compare Munir Mourra’s promise with Jacky Lumarque’s experience.

MPH proposes a campus for at least 4,000 students, technical-centre financing and a school inventory. These are Mourra’s party proposals. Places are not graduates already employed, and the passage is not the campus’s operating budget. [1] [2]

Université Quisqueya identifies Lumarque as rector since 2007, following INFP roles dating to the 1980s. That provides institutional experience to examine. His university’s biography is not itself an audit of fees, degree quality or graduates’ employment. [3]

Both require three accounts from different starting points: construction cost, annual expenditure and families’ burden. Mourra must explain an undelivered project; Lumarque can supply results from an institution he leads. A title does not guarantee quality; a building does not guarantee access.

Students should be able to learn the full cost of completing training, available financial help and what the qualification enables them to do. Those answers bring a large campus and a long career into the same debate about real opportunity.
