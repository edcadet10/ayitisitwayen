Hans Joseph and Badio: what the May 2021 employment record establishes

An ULCC statement put employment, departure and complaint dates on record. It does not turn past employment into proof of conspiracy.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Before Jovenel Moïse’s assassination, ULCC had an administrative matter concerning Joseph Félix Badio. After the killing, the institution published dates about it. Examining Hans Joseph’s record requires reading what those dates establish without adding conclusions they do not supply.

In the statement reproduced by Juno7 on July 13, 2021, ULCC says Badio joined March 11, 2013, ceased being staff on May 17, 2021 and was the subject of a complaint sent to Port-au-Prince prosecutors on May 20. It gave serious ethical breaches as dismissal grounds. This is ULCC’s account of its actions. [1]

Joseph had led ULCC since 2020, according to AyiboPost’s account of his 2026 departure. These dates do not establish that he recruited Badio in 2013 or knew of an assassination plot. Sharing an institution must remain distinct from each person’s knowledge, decisions or conduct. [2]

The documentary trail is more precise: the disciplinary decision, supporting evidence and prosecutors’ receipt of the complaint. The public statement does not supply the entire file. Without it, we cannot establish complete follow-up or concealment.

As a candidate, Joseph can explain how an institution he led handled an employee case and followed a complaint. That concerns administration and accountability. It is no license to guess his thoughts or treat one former employee as evidence against all colleagues.
