Jerry Tardieu vs Olivier Barrau: business experience must show whom it serves

A hotel and an insurer received international financing. Comparing the records means following the money to the people it was intended to help.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Running a business can prepare someone to make major decisions. A president, however, serves citizens who are not his shareholders. Jerry Tardieu’s and Olivier Barrau’s records allow us to examine that difference through two documented financing operations.

IFC’s 2011 annual report identifies Tardieu as Oasis Hotel’s chief executive. It describes a US$7.5 million loan that helped attract another US$15 million from investors. This was project lending, not a personal gift or an election contribution. [1]

For AIC, the IDB register shows a US$2 million loan approved in March 2012 within an US$8 million operation. The private-sector financing had no sovereign guarantee. The IDB described an aim of expanding insurance for lower-income people after the 2010 earthquake. AIC’s 2022 report identifies Barrau as chief executive. [2] [3] [4]

Loan size does not rank management ability. The projects, years and services differ. For the hotel, assessment needs jobs created, working conditions and local purchasing. For the insurer, it needs customer prices, coverage and claims paid. A financier’s stated purpose cannot substitute for those outcomes.

The other test concerns public power: what financial interests does each candidate retain, and which state decisions would he leave to others to prevent personal interests influencing them? These sources supply neither complete current asset declarations nor proof of an unlawful favour. They provide a starting point for examining business outcomes and the safeguards each candidate would place between business and government.
