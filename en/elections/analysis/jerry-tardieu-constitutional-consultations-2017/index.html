Jerry Tardieu and the Constitution: whom did the 2017 consultations reach?

A communiqué records meetings, but cannot replace submissions and decisions.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In an October 30, 2017 communiqué, the Chamber of Deputies’ constitutional-amendment commission described its consultations. The text published by Juno7 identifies Jerry Tardieu as chair and lists business, media, justice, universities and oversight institutions among participating sectors. [1]

The communiqué also announced further steps, including workshops across the ten departments and diaspora. What the commission reports doing differs from what it planned. An agenda is not a completion report. [1]

A consultation becomes useful when the public can trace an idea: who proposed it, the reasons offered, and why it was retained or rejected. A list of participating organizations cannot by itself show who shaped the final text.

Readers should seek submissions, synthesis reports and subsequent votes. Those would show whether a commune’s or citizen group’s concerns entered the changes. Absence from this communiqué does not prove a group never participated.

For a candidate presenting reform experience, the issue extends beyond meeting totals to how meetings changed a readable decision. That turns participation from an official photograph into a public record.
