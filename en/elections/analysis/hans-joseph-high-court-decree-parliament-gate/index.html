The decree Hans Joseph criticized: which gate must a case pass?

The December 2025 text places a parliamentary step in the prosecution of certain senior officials. Its own preamble acknowledges an inoperative legislature.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Hans Joseph told AyiboPost he criticized the High Court of Justice decree before leaving ULCC. The text explains the stakes: Le Moniteur of December 17, 2025 sets out a special procedure for presidents, prime ministers, ministers and other listed senior officials. [1] [2]

Articles 2 and 5 require a two-thirds Chamber of Deputies decision before the Senate sits as the High Court. Articles 12–13 reserve official-function offences to that court while leaving unrelated offences to ordinary courts. Article 4 uses broader wording about acts during office; that tension requires legal examination. [2]

The text itself identifies the practical problem: its preamble invokes an inoperative legislature. If a judicial route requires a closed parliamentary gate, gathering evidence alone cannot move a case through it. Rights organizations therefore challenged the measure as a threat to accountability. [2] [3]

This does not declare every procedural protection unlawful or every former official guilty, nor determine the decree’s effect in a particular case. The concrete question for past and aspiring leaders is which mechanism allows justice to examine power when political institutions are blocked.
