Martelly in the Moïse inquiry in July 2026: testimony is not conviction

A judicial appearance is a specific event. It should be kept distinct from other allegations or sanctions concerning the former president.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On July 20, 2026, Michel Martelly appeared before investigating judge Jean Denis Cyprien in the Jovenel Moïse assassination inquiry. Le National reports that it formed part of supplementary investigation ordered by the Court of Appeal. This was a Haitian investigative step, not a verdict in the US federal trial. [1]

Lawyer Mario Delcy told the newspaper his client answered questions as a witness and cooperated with the judge. That description of status is attributed to the lawyer. The public report does not supply the complete hearing record or every question asked. [1]

The distinctions matter. A witness can assist an investigation without being accused. An accusation requires its evidence and procedure; conviction requires adjudication. The July appearance establishes neither that Martelly organized the assassination nor an acquittal in every other matter concerning him.

Voters should follow what the investigation produces: verified information, resolved contradictions and evidence-based decisions. Treating a courthouse appearance or lawyer’s statement as the final outcome obscures the work remaining. This examines the July hearing, not the entire current docket.
