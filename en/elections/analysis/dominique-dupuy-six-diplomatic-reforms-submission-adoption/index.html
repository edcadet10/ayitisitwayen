Dupuy’s six reform texts: the distance between submitting and changing the rules

Her own August 2024 wording describes possible cabinet adoption. That qualification matters when judging a governing record.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On August 5, 2024, Dupuy’s account listed six legal instruments submitted for possible adoption by the Council of Ministers. The list covers diplomatic reform, an arts-and-letters order, ministry organisation, a diplomatic academy, career rules and diplomatic or official passports. [1]

The central word is possible. Submission is a documented claim about a step in a process. It is not an adoption notice, a published legal text or evidence that officials began applying new rules. [1]

No complete draft package, original cabinet adoption record or corresponding gazette publication was recovered in this review. That absence does not establish rejection or prove that no reform ever took effect. The legal status remains unresolved here.

The useful follow-up is specific. Which texts were adopted, on what dates and with what changes? Where can citizens read them? Which appointment, training or passport procedures changed? What budget and oversight accompanied implementation?

A reform announcement can identify a minister’s priorities. A governing record needs the next documents in the chain. Voters should be able to move from a promise, to an adopted rule, to a result they can inspect.
