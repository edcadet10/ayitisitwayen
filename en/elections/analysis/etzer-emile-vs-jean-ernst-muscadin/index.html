Etzer Emile vs Jean Ernest Muscadin: more police, accountable to whom?

One offers police recruitment targets. The other has a prosecutorial record that raises questions about the use of force. Security requires examining both.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A road free of gangs should not become a road where anyone must fear state agents. Comparing Etzer Emile and Jean Ernest Muscadin means examining two things together: the capacity to protect people and control over those who carry weapons for the state.

Emile’s programme proposes increasing operational police from about 13,000 to 26,000 by 2033. It promises faster recruitment and training of 1,000–2,000 officers during the first 100 days, alongside recruit screening, sanctions for abuse and a stronger inspectorate. Both the baseline and targets come from the campaign. [1]

Muscadin brings a record as a prosecutor. Demonstrators in Miragoâne portrayed him as a defender against gangs in November 2025. But BINUH’s April–June 2026 report cites his possible involvement in at least 76 summary executions documented for 2022–2025, saying no judicial proceedings had begun against him for them. [2] [3]

These records measure different things. Emile presents a plan; its targets are not results. The UN report examines alleged past conduct; it is not a criminal conviction. We do not have a common measure for both candidates that would establish who has protected more people.

The practical distinction becomes clearer. More recruits are insufficient without training, pay and supervision. One individual’s reputation does not show how a nationwide system would function. Both need to explain who checks a suspect’s identity, who investigates abuse and how a victim’s family obtains justice. Protection and accountability meet at that point.
