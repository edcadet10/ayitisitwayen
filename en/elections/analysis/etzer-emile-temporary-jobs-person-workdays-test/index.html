Etzer Emile: how long would 50,000 temporary jobs last?

People employed and days worked are different measures. A simple example shows why duration changes the promise’s cost and value.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Fifty thousand people finding work is a large figure. For a household, the next question comes quickly: for how many days? Without duration, neither household income nor the state’s wage cost can be assessed.

Etzer Emile’s jobs page proposes 50,000 temporary public-works jobs, separately from 300,000 formal jobs by 2033. We do not count people twice or treat temporary work as an established multiyear wage. [1]

Consider an illustration, not a campaign timetable: 50,000 people working 20 days produce one million person-workdays; 60 days produce three million. The headcount is unchanged, but workdays and wages triple at the same daily rate.

A World Bank PARSA paper reports 83,748 public-works person-workdays. That does not establish unique workers or permanent jobs. It illustrates why employment reporting needs precise units. [2]

A measurable commitment would state workers, days per worker, wages, payment dates and the asset left after construction. Households could assess income while the public compares spending with delivered services.
