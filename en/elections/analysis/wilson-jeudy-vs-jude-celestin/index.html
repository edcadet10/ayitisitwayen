Wilson Jeudy vs Jude Célestin: public works must account for the people affected

Delmas camp clearances and CNE equipment purchases are different records. They offer two ways to examine authority, resources and public service.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Clearing an area or buying heavy machinery can look like decisive action. Effects on people and a traceable spending record are also part of the outcome. Wilson Jeudy’s and Jude Célestin’s records allow examination of those two aspects.

Amnesty International reports more than 300 families evicted from two Delmas camps in May 2011, with shelters and belongings destroyed. Jeudy was mayor. Haïti en Marche defended the mayor’s security responsibilities and criticized the central state’s and other organizations’ handling of displacement. [1] [2]

Legal organization BAI announced a complaint against Jeudy. That is not a conviction; the reviewed records supplied no final decision on that complaint. A security rationale does not establish what notice families received, where else they could go or how they could challenge the operation. [3]

For Célestin, the Senate PetroCaribe commission revisited CNE equipment purchases during his leadership. Its 2016 report records an US$80 million allocation and says it lacked a complete inventory. One contract-table entry shows 81 trucks delivered instead of 100, with US$2,565,000 deducted. Calling the other 19 missing would erase the adjustment the table records. [4]

The parliamentary inquiry is not a conviction of Célestin. This comparison does not equate the two records. It gives each candidate a specific test: Jeudy must explain procedures and protection for people affected by municipal operations; Célestin must connect contracts and payments to equipment that exists and serves the public. Public action warrants assessment through service delivered and limits respected, beyond an image of strength.

Sources and verification limits

  1. Amnesty on Delmas evictions and threats in 2011

    Amnesty International · 2011-09-15

    AMR 36/013/2011, two pages. Distinguishes the May evictions from September threats, for which municipal authorities denied responsibility.

  2. Debate over the Delmas camps: pages 1 and 7

    Haïti en Marche / University of Florida archive · 2011-06-01

    The article defends the security rationale and criticizes the handling of displacement by the State and organizations. It is the newspaper’s position, not a judgment.

  3. BAI announces a complaint against the Delmas mayor

    Bureau des Avocats Internationaux / IJDH · 2011-05-31

    The release announces a complaint planned for June 1, 2011. It is neither proof of a conviction nor a copy of a final judgment.

  4. Senate commission’s 2016 PetroCaribe report

    Senate of Haiti / public report copy

    Public 98-page copy. For CNE, PDF pages 34–37 cover the budget, purchase table, testimony and inventory limitation. A parliamentary inquiry recommending further investigation, not a conviction.
