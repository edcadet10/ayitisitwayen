Jude Elie vs Olivier Barrau: a company name is not an asset declaration

Entrepreneurial biographies, partner titles and a company balance sheet supply different information. Comparing them requires preserving each document’s limits.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A candidate can name several firms; another can point to a large balance sheet. Neither alone establishes personal ownership, debt or interests during a term. That is the starting point for comparing Jude Elie and Olivier Barrau.

Elie names construction, energy and petroleum businesses in his own biography. Million Billion Group presents him as an infrastructure and development-strategy partner. Those pages document how his roles are presented, not a shareholder register with dated ownership percentages. [1] [2]

AIC’s 2022 report identifies Barrau as board president and publishes a balance sheet and audit letter. Assets are approximately 4.206 billion gourdes and liabilities 2.755 billion. These are company accounts, not the candidate’s entire estate. All shareholders’ equity is not automatically his share. [3] [4]

Not locating a public register proves neither that a business is fictitious nor that someone concealed a crime. An audit’s existence likewise does not settle all personal interests. The comparison concerns what different documents allow readers to verify.

Both can provide current interests, dates of ended relationships and rules preventing state decisions from favoring connected firms. That places business experience in a chain citizens can follow: interests, decision-makers and oversight.
