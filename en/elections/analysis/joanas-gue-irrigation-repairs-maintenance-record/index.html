Joanas Gué and 115 irrigation systems: repairs need a follow-through

An old statement on agricultural works opens a record of maintenance and outcomes.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In an interview reported by Métropole on January 7, 2009, Joanas Gué said emergency funds had been used to repair 115 irrigation systems after the 2008 hurricanes. He was agriculture minister. This is his account, not our audit of each system. [1]

Why revisit an old number? An irrigation system serves farmers beyond the day work ends. It needs water, clear canals and assigned maintenance. Without follow-through, a repair may fail to protect the next harvest.

Assessing the work starts with names and locations of the 115 systems, planned works, payments and acceptance dates. A second record would identify maintenance responsibility and user participation. A national total cannot replace those details.

Assessment must also consider later disasters, changes of administration and available resources. A system failing today does not by itself establish that work in 2009 never occurred or money was stolen.

The earlier responsibility still matters. A candidate promising agricultural recovery can show what experience taught about maintenance, beyond launching projects. That separates an announced work from a service farmers can rely on.
