Jude Elie’s Del Rio statement: what the later investigation qualified

The agency’s findings describe dangerous conduct while rejecting evidence of people being struck with reins. Both findings belong in the account.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A September 22,2021 statement published by Jude Elie’s account condemns horseback pursuit of Haitian migrants and demands investigation. Its image carries the PPSL/Sove Lonè header and his printed name. It refers to whips, but does not explicitly establish that reins struck people. [1]

DHS later told the U.S. Senate that its investigation found dangerous behaviour, denigrating language and force or threats that drove migrants toward the river. It also reported no evidence migrants were struck by reins. The findings were released July 8,2022, after Elie’s statement, despite the 2021 hearing date in the answer file’s name. [2]

One finding does not cancel the other. Rejecting evidence of a specific kind of physical contact does not erase the recorded dangerous conduct. Conversely, documented misconduct does not establish every description in an early political statement. A faithful account can retain the concern and correct the detail.

The Senate answers summarise the agency’s own inquiry. They are not the complete investigation, a criminal verdict or a verified account of every later disciplinary outcome. The source does not establish that Elie knew the later findings when he spoke.

The follow-through question for a candidate is whether public advocacy stays accurate as evidence develops. Did the platform update its account, document protection cases or identify concrete assistance? Those records would show what followed the declaration without turning either an agency summary or political outrage into a complete judicial account.
