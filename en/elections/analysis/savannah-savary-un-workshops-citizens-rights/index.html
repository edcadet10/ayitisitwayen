Savannah Savary: a civic-work record predating the campaign

A UN archive documents writing workshops in 2018. Their themes—rights, justice and security—return in this election.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A United Nations archive identifies Savannah Savary and writer Frankétienne as facilitators helping pupils write fables. It dates the final day of an eight-day workshop to July 3, 2018 and describes participation from 18 Port-au-Prince schools. [1]

The themes extended beyond writing a good story. They included human rights, the rule of law and security. The account and photograph come from MINUJUSTH, the former UN mission supporting justice in Haiti. [1]

This supplies a concrete work record beyond a general statement of commitment. The archive does not measure what pupils retained years later or how the workshop affected their lives. Participation and demonstrated long-term results require different evidence.

The political connection is worth examining. Experience helping young people discuss rights can inform a security debate. Voters can also ask what her presidential program would do when a citizen’s rights conflict with an operation the authorities want to conduct.

Where could a young person complain about a State agent? How would they learn that in a language they understand? The 2018 work offers a starting point for those questions. A campaign drawing on that experience should show how rights become usable in everyday life.
