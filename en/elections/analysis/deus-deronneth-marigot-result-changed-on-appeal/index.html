Déus Deronneth in Marigot: why the first result was not the last word

An old parliamentary contest shows what a challenge can change, and what it cannot establish.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

According to the EU mission’s 2016 election report, examination of 34 legislative challenges reversed just one deputy result: Marigot. This is the observers’ account of the process. [1]

Rezo Nòdwès reported in January 2017 that the final decision gave PONT candidate Déus Deronneth victory over Dieudonné Lhérisson, who had ranked ahead in preliminary results. Reports of subsequent violence do not establish that Deronneth ordered any act. [2]

Understanding such a result requires the electoral decision and changes to polling records. An outlet naming the winner does not automatically explain which votes were counted, excluded or corrected. We do not present that article as the judgment itself.

This matters for people following the present race. Initial totals may face challenges, which need deadlines, grounds and accessible decisions. Winning a challenge does not automatically imply fraud; filing one does not automatically establish merit.

In Deronneth’s career, Marigot identifies a specific step into office. It calls for the records that changed the result, rather than a legend about how elections always work.
