Olivier Barrau and farmers’ insurance: when rainfall determines payment

An AIC–WFP project offers a concrete way to examine the candidate’s experience: whom does insurance protect, and under what conditions?

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A farmer who loses a harvest needs money to plant again. In 2022, AIC, whose board president was Olivier Barrau, launched an insurance pilot with the World Food Programme, WRMS and SCOR. WFP reports 4,990 smallholders across 11 communes in Grand’Anse and Sud, 51% of them women. [1] [2]

Coverage addressed excess rainfall and rainfall deficit. WFP gives a maximum of about US$900 per farmer, divided equally between the two risks. That is not a promise of US$900 whenever a harvest fails. A coverage ceiling and an actual payout are different things. [2]

This kind of insurance uses a predefined measure, such as rainfall, to trigger payment. IFC explains its central weakness: the index may not match an individual’s losses. A farmer can suffer serious damage without receiving a payment that covers it. That is a design risk, not evidence that this AIC pilot rejected a particular claim. [3]

The political test is whether a system protects people with little financial cushion. A useful record would show how many received payouts, how quickly and what participants understood before enrolling. Enrolment matters; the conditions and results of protection matter just as much.
