Opinion: the food crisis needs two campaign calendars

We should assess what feeds a household now alongside what improves its livelihood over several years.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A mother looking for food today lives on a different clock from a major plan for 2033. We believe campaigns owe two answers together: how a household gets through the next months and how it becomes less dependent on relief.

IPC estimates 5.94 million people face Crisis-level acute food insecurity or worse in September 2026–February 2027. It projects 6.35 million for March–June 2027, out of 11.2 million analysed. The latter is a projection, not an observed count for those future months. [1]

Etzer Emile proposes 2033 employment targets and temporary work in his first hundred days. MPH proposes agricultural centres and energy investment. It would be unfair to say candidates discuss only distant goals. Voters still need the bridge between immediate relief and lasting change. [2] [3] [4]

We would require a simple first list: which households receive support, how much, when and how to complain if it fails to arrive. Then a second list for water, roads, markets and lasting work. Both need costs and accountable officials.

A meal cannot replace an economy. An economic promise cannot replace today’s meal either. A campaign taking people’s lives seriously should explain both without postponing every answer to a distant year.
