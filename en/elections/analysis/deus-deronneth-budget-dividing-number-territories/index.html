Deronneth’s budget: the number beneath the division sign

His posted amounts can be reproduced. Changing the number of eligible territories changes the cheque each would receive.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Deus Deronneth’s April 2024 graphics assign 60% of the budget to central government, 16% to communal sections, 14% to communes and 10% to departments. A second graphic posts amounts for each territorial unit using the 2023–2024 budget. [1] [2]

The initial state budget prints a global envelope of 320.645 billion gourdes. Taking the proposed shares and dividing by 571 sections, 146 communes and ten departments reproduces the posted rounded amounts: about 89.85 million, 307.47 million and 3.20645 billion gourdes respectively. [3] [1] [2]

That is a reconstruction of the arithmetic, not proof that Deronneth explicitly used those divisors. It identifies the question his graphic leaves readers to solve: which geographic list determines who gets paid?

IHSI’s later publication of 2024 population estimates counts 574 communal sections, 149 communes and ten departments. Using that list with the same budget and shares produces about 89.38 million per section and 301.28 million per commune. The departmental amount stays the same. [4] [3]

The later list does not establish which administrative boundaries applied to the April proposal. These are allocations in a model, not evidence that the treasury made transfers. The global budget is an authorised envelope, not actual spending.

A workable proposal needs a dated list of eligible units and a rule for changes to that list. Otherwise, creating or recognising another commune changes everyone’s share without voters seeing the mechanism. The division sign is small; the administrative decision underneath it moves millions.
