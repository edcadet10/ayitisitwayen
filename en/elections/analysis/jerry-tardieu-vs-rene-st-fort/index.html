Jerry Tardieu vs René St-Fort: speaking with a citizen is not their signature of support

Constitutional consultation and party membership serve different functions. Both need a record of what the citizen actually agreed to.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Someone may attend a meeting to criticize an idea rather than support it. A name in a database likewise does not explain the commitment made. That distinction connects two records involving Jerry Tardieu and René St-Fort.

In October 2017, the constitutional-amendment commission chaired by Tardieu issued a communiqué on consultations with business, media, justice and universities. It also announced workshops in ten departments and the diaspora. Reported activity and announced steps must remain distinct. [1]

In 2026, Vant Bèf Info reported René St-Fort’s PRN challenging the 30,000-supporter condition and seeking suspension of the process. That documents the party’s position; it establishes neither a falsified list nor consent from everyone on any list. [2]

Consultation requires knowing what someone proposed and how the authority used it. Membership requires knowing whether the person agreed to registration and use of their name. The records are not the same legal procedure; comparing them exposes the limits of what a name establishes.

For Tardieu, a useful record shows which views changed the text. For St-Fort, it shows who gave a party authority to speak for them. Participation should not become a number carrying a meaning the citizen never gave it.
