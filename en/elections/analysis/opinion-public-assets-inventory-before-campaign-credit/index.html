Opinion: a public machine needs more than a photograph

We want inventories connecting payments, equipment and service. That protects public money without convicting anyone from a photograph.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A photograph of a public machine establishes at least that a machine faced a camera. It does not establish ownership, fair pricing or continuing operation. We believe inventories belong in election debate as a service issue.

The CNE record during Jude Célestin’s period raises equipment and inventory questions. An ULCC account from the institution Hans Joseph led describes drone images showing no announced houses on a site. These are different files, not evidence of a shared operation. [1] [2]

PARSA’s June 2026 paper gives another practical reason: an incomplete asset inventory alongside control weaknesses. That is no accusation against these two candidates over PARSA management. It shows the relevance of the same control tool elsewhere. [3]

We would require an entry for major equipment: identification, purchase date, cost, service location, custodian and maintenance. Public detail should protect security where necessary. Accountability does not require publishing every sensitive movement.

Combined with physical checks, that entry lets people follow purchases. A missing record requires investigation rather than an automatic theft finding. Without functioning records, a country pays without being able to follow what it bought.
