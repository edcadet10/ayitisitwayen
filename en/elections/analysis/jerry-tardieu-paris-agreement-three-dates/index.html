Tardieu and the Paris Agreement: three dates, three different steps

A parliamentary announcement and an international ratification notice can both be accurate without describing the same event.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On February 1, 2017, Jerry Tardieu’s account said the decree ratifying the Paris climate agreement had just been adopted in the National Assembly. That is a claim about a domestic parliamentary action. [1]

The United Nations depositary notice records Haiti’s international ratification on July 31, 2017, followed by entry into force on August 30. It does not purport to date every earlier parliamentary step. [2]

Reading the July date as proof that the February announcement was false would confuse two procedures. Domestic approval, depositing the country’s instrument internationally and the agreement taking effect are different events. An apparent calendar contradiction can disappear when the document’s purpose is read.

The original domestic decree, session minutes, quorum record and individual roll call were not recovered in this review. The UN notice therefore supports the international stage; it does not independently verify Tardieu’s vote, attendance or contribution.

For a candidate presenting a climate record, the next questions go beyond the calendar. Which commitments became domestic measures? Which projects received money, who implemented them and what changed for exposed communities? Ratification can open a policy path. It is not itself a payment or a completed protective work.
