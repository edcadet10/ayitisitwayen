Etzer Emile’s school budget: 16% and 22% measure different things

Money authorized on paper does not always equal money delivered to a service.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Etzer Emile’s education programme gives two targets: education would receive 16% of public spending actually executed in 2029, and 22% of the national budget in 2033. The first concerns execution; the second concerns the budget. [1]

For a parent, these may sound like the same promise. A budget authorizes expenditure; execution records spending that occurs. If the state authorizes 22 of every 100 gourdes for schools but does not release the money, the paper percentage cannot pay a teacher.

An example of the tracking problem appears in the state audit for fiscal 2023–2024: the Court of Accounts criticized inclusion of FNE revenue in the government balance while its expenditure was omitted. This does not prove Etzer’s promise impossible; it shows why definitions and accounts matter. [2]

To compare 2029 with 2033, the campaign should give both measures each year: amounts authorized, released and spent. It should name the institutions included, so an accounting change cannot masquerade as classroom progress.

Ultimately, a parent wants a teacher present, an open school and affordable fees. The percentage helps only when the public can trace the money to those outcomes.
