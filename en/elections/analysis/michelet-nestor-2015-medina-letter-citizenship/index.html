Michelet Nestor in 2015: defending people of Haitian descent beyond a slogan

His letter to Danilo Medina records a concrete demand. It also highlights the distinction between a person’s ancestry and legal status.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On June 24, 2015, presidential candidate Michelet Nestor’s office published a letter to Dominican President Danilo Medina and the immigration director. It sought a halt to planned mass expulsions and an accessible path to citizenship, emphasizing that many affected people had never visited Haiti. [1]

This was the candidate’s position, not a decision granting residence or citizenship. The letter criticized Dominican policy and sought change. It is not used here as guidance on immigration rules applicable today. [1]

For voters, the lasting issue is precision. Haitian ancestry alone does not establish birthplace, citizenship or rights. A policy treating everyone identically can miss different situations. Defending people requires understanding those distinctions rather than relying solely on a shared-ancestry appeal.

The letter records a commitment made before Nestor’s return to the current electoral process. Evaluating follow-through requires evidence of subsequent initiatives, partners mobilized and outcomes obtained. Publishing a demand and securing change for the affected people are different stages.
