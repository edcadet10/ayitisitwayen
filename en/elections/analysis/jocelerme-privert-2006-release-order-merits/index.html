Jocelerme Privert: three pages explaining the limits of his 2006 release

The Gonaïves decision grants release subject to appearance. It also says the application does not open a merits examination.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Leaving prison and obtaining a final decision on allegations are different things. Jocelerme Privert’s 2006 record shows why the document matters. The three-page copy preserved by IJDH supplies the decision’s limits rather than leaving readers to guess what released means.

The Gonaïves Court of Appeal’s first-section decision is dated June 13, 2006. It references the La Scierie matter in Saint-Marc and applications involving Privert and Yvon Neptune among others. That places the former minister in proceedings over serious allegations; these pages are not a conviction. [1]

Page 3 lifts Privert’s detention and arrest warrants, requiring his appearance when called for procedural acts. It expressly separates the release application from examination of the merits. This decision therefore cannot be sold as a verdict on all responsibility in La Scierie. [1]

Privert publicly rejected the allegations. Scoop FM reported in December 2009 that he said justice had cleared him. That belongs in the record as his response. We lack the complete judicial file to connect that account to another final decision; we do not assert that none exists. [2]

Sound scrutiny preserves the accused person’s right not to be labeled guilty without grounds and victims’ right not to have unresolved questions erased. A precise date and operative decision help voters more than a label exceeding the document.
