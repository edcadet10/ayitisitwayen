Etzer Emile: 2033 jobs and a trade window ending in 2028

The industrial programme runs beyond the HOPE/HELP extension. That calls for a plan for the years after 2028.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A factory does not hire for a single day. Investors need to know who can buy its products for years. Two calendars therefore deserve to meet in Etzer Emile’s campaign: his employment target and access to the U.S. market.

His programme targets 300,000 formal jobs by 2033, including 150,000 in four economic zones. It does not make them all textile jobs. Several industries are involved, so the entire programme cannot be reduced to one customs preference. [1]

However, the U.S. Senate Finance Committee’s September 3, 2026 announcement says H.R. 6500 extends HOPE/HELP through the end of 2028. These programmes provide duty-free U.S. access for certain Haitian textile and apparel products. The announcement promises no further extension. [2]

That does not doom the plan. It identifies a risk for qualifying export-dependent activities. A credible plan would separate jobs relying on that preference, jobs serving Haiti and alternative markets if rules change.

For a household, the good news is not merely a factory opening; it is a wage that lasts. Explaining 2029–2033 would help assess the promise without turning a campaign target into a guarantee from Washington.
