Gandhy Dorfeuille vs Munir Mourra: construction schedules differ from the hours electricity arrives

Saint-Louis du Sud in 2014 and a Jacmel record in 2026 provide two ways to examine an energy promise’s results.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An electricity-line launch and a plant on paper are different from power arriving nightly. Comparing Gandhy Dorfeuille and Munir Mourra means following committed money, completed works and delivered service. Their records concern different cities and periods.

Le Nouvelliste reported a second electrification phase in Saint-Louis du Sud in April 2014, naming deputy Dorfeuille among officials involved. Its public excerpt gives a 24-million-gourde budget and 18 million available. The six-million difference is not automatically missing money; it separates financing stages. [1]

For Mourra, MPH proposes plant rehabilitation and solar and wind development. An existing baseline matters: the World Bank’s October 2026 report puts Jacmel’s solar-and-battery works at approximately 35% completion. That is neither Mourra’s achievement nor a national electricity-access measure. [2] [3]

Dorfeuille’s historical record needs delivery documents and service evidence. Mourra’s proposal needs a project inventory and a plan connecting projects. Missing delivery records in an excerpt do not establish failure; a proposal does not establish financing.

The common test is household life: service hours, connection cost, bills and repairs. Money and concrete help when connected to that chain. It gives voters observable outcomes for comparing an old project and a new commitment.
