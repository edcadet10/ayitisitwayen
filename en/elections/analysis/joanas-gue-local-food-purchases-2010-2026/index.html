Joanas Gué called for local buying in 2010. School meals offer a test today.

An idea can recur across campaigns. The purchasing system determines delivery.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

After the 2010 earthquake, agriculture minister Joanas Gué advocated redirecting food assistance towards purchases from Haitian producers rather than relying on imports. Métropole reported that position on March 15, 2010. [1]

In June 2026, a WFP official reported its school-meal programme had grown from 450,000 to 650,000 children over two years. Nearly three-quarters of those meals were locally produced, up from half. These are WFP’s figures for its programme, not every canteen in Haiti. [2]

The sources do not establish that Gué’s 2010 appeal caused the 2026 results. They reveal an enduring question: how can food spending feed children while giving producers a market? Etzer Emile’s programme also proposes local purchasing for school meals. [1] [2] [3]

A farmer needs an agreed quantity, price and payment date. A school needs timely, safe food. Contracts, storage, transport and reliable payment connect those needs.

Candidate comparisons should therefore go deeper than “national production.” Who buys, from whom, under what rules and oversight? Without those answers, an attractive idea can recur at each election without expanding service.
