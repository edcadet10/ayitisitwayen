Opinion: a consultation needs a route to follow

We support following the care pathway: where patients go next, what they pay and who follows up.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A free consultation can bring relief. But care can stall when a doctor requests a test the household cannot afford. We believe campaigns should measure a patient’s route through care, not only consultation totals or entry prices.

MPH proposes mobile clinics and hospital-centre modernization. AIC reported that over half its sickness/death portfolio’s insured people paid at most US$10 monthly in 2021–2022. These are different evidence: a service proposal and a dated company indicator. [1] [2]

Neither the clinic promise nor premium alone establishes everything a patient needs to know about tests, medicine, transport and remaining charges. We do not say all are excluded; these two facts cannot establish complete protection.

We would ask each plan to publish a clearly hypothetical patient example: first visit, tests, treatment, charges and follow-up. No invented testimony—just service terms on paper. A household could see where access exists and where further support is needed.

That gives no single model a monopoly on good health policy. It offers a simple measure: once I enter care, is there a real path to the treatment I need?

Sources and verification limits

  1. MPH manifesto — health and security on page 9, economy and finance on page 10

    Mobilisation pour le Progrès d’Haïti

    The 14-page document was reviewed. These are party proposals, not a budget law or proof of available funding.

  2. AIC: social indicators on health/death premiums, pages 32–33

    Alternative Insurance Company

    Company statement for 2021–2022: 53% of insured people in its sickness/death portfolio pay at most US$10 monthly. Not every product, family or November 2026 price. The tables do not establish coverage of all care costs.
