Kely Bastien in 2010: what limits should unelected power face?

After the earthquake, he discussed deadlines and legitimacy. That position belongs in the political record.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In March 2010, Haiti was considering how to renew its institutions after the earthquake, with René Préval’s term approaching its end. Senate president Kelly Bastien urged political actors to examine the option closest to the Constitution, according to Radio Métropole. [1]

He did not rule out a provisional government. But he emphasized efforts to hold an election within 90 days and criticized giving an administration without popular legitimacy a one-year mandate. This is a reported position, not a deadline we claim he enacted. [1]

The dilemma is straightforward: a crisis may prevent an election on time, but those managing it do not receive unlimited permission to remain. Deadlines should identify what must happen, who is responsible and how the public can check progress.

An old statement does not prove he resolved that crisis or automatically answer every later situation. It does establish a principle Bastien advanced while holding high public office.

As a candidate today, he can explain what safeguards he would accept to keep exceptions temporary: public accounts of delays, independent oversight and consequences for missed commitments. Demanding limits on others matters most when one accepts those limits on one’s own power.
