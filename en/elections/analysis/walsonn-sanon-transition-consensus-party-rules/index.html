Walsonn Sanon: rejecting a pact, then building consent

Between 2022 and 2025, he challenged political selection and party practices. Those standards also offer a way to examine his own organization.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On December 27, 2022, Scoop FM reported that SOLID leader Walsonn Sanon rejected the December 21 agreement. He said his organization’s reservations had received promises of revision, but he then learned through social media that signing would proceed. He sought broader consent rather than rejecting any agreement in principle. [1]

He also criticized the selection process for Mirlande Manigat in the HCT, the High Transition Council associated with the 2022 agreement. That body differs from the Transitional Presidential Council, or CPT, formed in 2024. Combining them would blur two separate arrangements. [1]

In a May 4, 2025 Rezo Nòdwès interview, Sanon, identified as ANFÒS coordinator, accused parties of serving personal privilege instead of connecting citizens to the state. He called for new leadership rooted in local life. Those are his political assessments, not findings from an investigation of every party. [2]

Both positions turn on who participates in decisions. For voters, the useful test is ANFÒS’s own rules: selecting leaders, handling disagreement and explaining decisions to members. Criticism of exclusion at state level becomes more persuasive when the critic’s organization makes its own methods visible.
