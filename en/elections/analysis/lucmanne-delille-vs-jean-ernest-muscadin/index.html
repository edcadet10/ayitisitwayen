Lucmanne Delille vs Jean Ernest Muscadin: an officer’s rights and limits on power belong together

A 2020 police-union decision and a 2023 jurisdictional reprimand illuminate two sides of public service.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A state officer needs rights to defend working conditions. Citizens need rights to challenge the officer’s actions. Comparing Lucmanne Delille and Jean Ernest Muscadin means examining those needs together without inventing a personal conflict.

The March 10, 2020 PNH personnel order reproduced by HaitiLibre bears Justice Minister Delille’s and Prime Minister Joseph Jouthe’s names. It recognizes space to associate and organize in unions to defend rights under legal conditions. It does not make Delille sole author of all police union rights. [1]

In December 2023, Juno7 reported a justice-ministry letter reminding Muscadin of jurisdiction and accountability limits. It criticized intervention in another jurisdiction without authorization and a roadblock supporters mounted in his name. This is a supervising authority’s reprimand, not a criminal conviction. [2]

Organizing rights do not authorize unchecked power. Discipline should not become an excuse to erase workplace rights. The records involve different years and responsibilities; one does not establish that Delille issued the order concerning Muscadin in the other.

Voters can ask which complaint channels both would protect: for an officer mistreated at work and for a citizen alleging official abuse. Losing either removes a check on public service. Security needs personnel treated fairly and held accountable.
