Etzer Émile: a late budget and promises needing evidence

In 2020, the economist questioned the credibility of the government’s timetable and revenue forecasts. The same test can examine his own promises.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On June 10, 2020, Juno7 published Etzer Émile’s analysis of the 2019–2020 budget. He highlighted a straightforward problem: cabinet adopted it on June 5 for a year running from October 1, 2019 to September 30, 2020. Most of the period being planned had already elapsed. [1]

He also tested whether forecast money would arrive. He compared revenue already collected with the faster pace needed to reach the annual target and questioned whether expected international support could arrive before year-end. These were challenges to forecasts, not findings about the year’s final results. [1]

The lesson is accessible: a budget must connect achievable work, likely income and available time. A figure on paper does not, by itself, finance a school or hospital. Expected revenue, actual receipts and spending must remain distinguishable.

For Émile as a candidate, his earlier analysis supplies a useful standard. Promises funded through new revenue or foreign assistance merit the same questions: which source, what timetable and what changes if the money fails to arrive? His 2020 criticism does not finance his current programme; it shows how to scrutinize its financing.
