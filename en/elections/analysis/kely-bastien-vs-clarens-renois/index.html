Kely Bastien vs Clarens Renois: leaders’ agreement is not every citizen’s mandate

DELIVRANS nomination and 2015 Protestant negotiations require understanding who could commit whom.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

An organization can select a candidate without owning the votes of everyone it says it represents. That reflects voters’ freedom. Kely Bastien’s nomination record and a past agreement favoring Clarens Renois show why a mandate’s boundaries need to be visible.

Reporting identifies Bastien as DELIVRANS’s presidential choice. That records a political structure’s decision, not agreement by every supporter or disappearance of other views. A useful record explains the selecting body and decision process. [1]

A 2015 Rezo Nòdwès report describes Protestant-candidate negotiations resulting in Renois’s selection. Other actors’ participation did not turn churches into one ballot. This is a historical episode, not a map of all Renois’s 2026 alliances. [2]

The decisions involve different years and groups. Both warrant information about decision-makers and room for dissent. Religious principle or political affiliation should not erase an individual’s freedom to compare programmes.

For Bastien and Renois, public debate means explaining selection rules and accepting questions from people outside the room. An agreement can launch a campaign. It does not replace the reasons a citizen needs to cast their own vote.

    Fact Checking News (FCN) · 2026-11-14

    Reports the communiqué describing the November 13 deliberation. An internal selection is not a national election or final CEP approval.
