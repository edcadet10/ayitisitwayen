Etzer Emile and the 40% women’s credit target: whom do we count, what money do we follow?

A beneficiary target is not automatically a loan-amount target. BRH’s figures require additional distinctions.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

If four in ten programme beneficiaries are women, do they receive four in ten gourdes too? Not necessarily. That is a logical example, not an evaluated programme result. It shows what Etzer Emile’s women-and-credit target must define.

The campaign sets women at 40% of micro, small and medium enterprise credit beneficiaries in 2033. It places the target alongside equipped markets, storage, freight transport and access to savings and insurance for madan sara traders. These are proposals, not counts of women already funded. [1]

BRH’s October–December 2025 note offers a different table, separating credit by gender and currency. Women appear with 37.40% of gourde credit and 23.51% of dollar credit. Those percentages cannot be merged or relabeled as beneficiary counts for Emile’s programme. [2]

The campaign needs to specify whether a beneficiary means a person, a business or each new loan. It also needs amounts, duration and borrowing costs. Otherwise one person could appear repeatedly, or one group receive many small loans while another receives larger sums.

This establishes no discrimination in a particular case. It provides a way to test inclusion: who gains an opportunity, on what terms and for how much? A useful measure helps a trader understand her own terms, beyond improving a report’s appearance.
