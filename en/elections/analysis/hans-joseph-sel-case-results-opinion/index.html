Hans Joseph’s SEL: count the path from investigation to justice

Security, the rule of law and fighting corruption are clear priorities. Citizens need to see how results would be measured.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

A November 16, 2026 post on Hans Joseph’s account presents SEL: security, the rule of law and fighting corruption. The complete poster and caption offer a campaign message, not a costed implementation plan. [1]

The initials are memorable. The harder question is what citizens would be able to check one year into government. A corruption complaint, an agency investigation, a file sent to prosecutors, a charge, a judgment and recovered money are different steps.

A credible scorecard would publish anonymous or legally public case totals at each step, with dates and definitions. It would explain delays and identify which institution controls the next decision. Counting only investigations can exaggerate success; counting only convictions can hide obstacles outside an investigator’s control.

The same rules should apply to political opponents and allies. Readers should see safeguards against selective investigations, protection for witnesses and a way to correct mistaken accusations. Security and legal limits belong in the same plan.

This is a proposed standard, not an audit of Joseph’s complete institutional record. That record still requires original case files, court outcomes and responses. A strong anti-corruption promise should make those distinctions easier for citizens to follow, not ask them to accept a slogan as a result.
