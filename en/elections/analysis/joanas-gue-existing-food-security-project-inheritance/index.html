Joanas Gué: the next president will not start agriculture from zero

PARSA already has funding, activities and reported weaknesses. They belong in scrutiny of a former agriculture minister.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A campaign can make every solution sound as though it begins after victory. Agriculture is more complicated. The next president inherits programmes, contracts and households already waiting for services.

The World Bank’s June 8, 2026 PARSA paper reports nearly 25 million school meals and 83,748 person-workdays in public works. Those are not 83,748 permanent jobs. It also reports producer support and rural infrastructure work. [1]

The same paper identifies incomplete supporting documents and asset inventories, plus weak internal controls. The Bank’s finance portal lists the additional US$50 million grant as approved and effective. Approval does not mean all funds have reached beneficiaries. [1] [2]

Joanas Gué was agriculture minister in 2007; that does not make him responsible for PARSA’s 2026 management. His experience does provide a starting point for specifics: what would he keep, which controls would he repair and how would delivery be verified? [3]

Our assessment: renaming a project is not agricultural policy. Delivering school meals, reaching farmers and accounting for spending would give households a record they can understand and check.

Sources and verification limits

    World Bank · 2026-06-08

    PDF pages 13–17 separate the existing project, reported results and proposed additional finance. Workdays are not permanent jobs. Control weaknesses are not a finding that a candidate stole money.
