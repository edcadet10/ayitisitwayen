Lumarque’s scholarship promise needs rules that outlive a benefactor

Personal educational help can change a life. A national policy must explain who qualifies, who pays and how selection stays fair.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An editorial argument. Its judgments are not official findings about candidates.

In July 2015, Lumarque’s account said around twenty Cerca Carvajal scholarship recipients had become professionals. In April 2026, it celebrated a student from La Saline. Education as a route to a different life connects the two messages. [1] [2]

The older number is approximate and self-reported. Independent aggregate graduation results, full funding records and selection rules were not recovered. That gap proves neither that the assistance failed nor that it bought political loyalty.

But voters should demand more than the portrait of a generous person. A public scholarship belongs to citizens under published rules. A student should know how to apply, how decisions are made, how to challenge an error and whether political allegiance can affect selection.

The same standard applies to every contender. Publish the budget, sources of money, eligibility rules and anonymous results. Explain the boundary between personal giving, university support and a programme financed by the state. Those are different responsibilities.

A scholarship can open a door. The presidential test is whether that door would remain open, on equal terms, after the benefactor leaves office. Gratitude is personal. Access to a public opportunity should not have to be.
