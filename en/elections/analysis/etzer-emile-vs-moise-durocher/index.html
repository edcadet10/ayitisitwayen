Etzer Emile vs Moïse Durocher: what counts as a job?

A small-business loan, temporary employment and a lasting job are different outcomes. Campaign totals need common definitions before comparison.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A young person needs to know whether work will provide regular pay, not simply whether their name enters a statistic. Etzer Emile’s and Moïse Durocher’s employment proposals show how large numbers can obscure that distinction.

Emile’s campaign jobs page proposes interest-free loans of one million gourdes each for 5,000 projects, alongside a guarantee fund. It places that measure within the first 100 days. It also sets targets of 50,000 temporary public-works jobs and 300,000 formal jobs by 2033. [1]

A November 12, 2026 profile gives Durocher’s goal of 1,500,000 direct jobs, connecting production and training in marine science, animal production, and water and ecosystems. That source supplies no precise deadline for the full total. We cannot attach 2033 to his figure as though he promised that date. [2]

The difference is not simply 1.5 million against 300,000. Job types and timeframes do not yet share a common basis. For Emile, 5,000 loans of one million gourdes equal five billion gourdes in loan value. That does not establish employment created or total state expenditure. For Durocher, the job total does not show how much work would be seasonal or provide year-round wages.

A sound comparison needs the same table from both: new paid positions, employers hiring, duration, training, cost and funding. Existing work must be separated from employment created. Otherwise a campaign can count a trainee as an employed person before that person has received a wage.
