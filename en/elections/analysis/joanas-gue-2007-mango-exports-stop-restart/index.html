Joanas Gué and mango exports in 2007: reopening did not end the crisis

An announced mid-July restart was followed by another suspension at month-end. Following the dates changes the assessment.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Mango exports to the United States were suspended July 2, 2007 after live fruit-fly larvae were found in shipments. On July 18, Radio Métropole reported Agriculture Secretary of State Joanas Gué’s announcement of a July 19 restart, following renewed authorization for six plants not implicated in the triggering shipments. [1]

By July 31, the station reported another suspension. Gué said the ministry, exporters’ association and USDA had agreed to stop shipments because of infestation levels. He announced a state-funded 25-million-gourde programme to reduce fruit flies in producing areas. [2]

This history does not establish fraud by the six plants or illegality of the initial authorization. It shows that reopening market access did not eliminate production-chain risks. Reopening a market and keeping it open are connected tasks requiring different checks.

Voters assessing Gué’s export record should examine what followed the announcements: field and processing controls, information for producers and programme outcomes. The two July reports do not provide that complete record. They prevent an announced restart from being mistaken for durable success.

Sources and verification limits

  1. Joanas Gué announces mango exports will resume July 19, 2007

    Radio Métropole · 2007-07-18

    Describes the July 2 suspension and renewed authorization for six plants. The reopening announcement is not an audit of every subsequent shipment.

  2. Renewed suspension of mango exports in late July 2007

    Radio Métropole · 2007-07-31

    Gué’s explanation of plant-health risks and an announced programme; not agricultural treatment advice or a judgment about every plant.
