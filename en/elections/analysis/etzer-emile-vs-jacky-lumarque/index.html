Etzer Emile vs Jacky Lumarque: who checks employment after the diploma?

Emile links employer-training support to employment entry. Lumarque’s record allows scrutiny of how training outcomes are measured over time.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A young person finishing training can have a certificate without a wage. What happens after the course matters. That is where Etzer Emile’s programme meets former vocational-training administrator Jacky Lumarque’s record.

Emile proposes certifying 100,000 young people in a trade by 2033. His programme targets at least 60% employment entry twelve months after graduation and proposes financial training support for firms according to employment-entry results. These are campaign targets and mechanisms, not verified student-cohort outcomes. [1]

Université Quisqueya’s biography dates Lumarque’s INFP deputy directorship to 1984, directorship to 1987 and rectorship to 2007. Those dates identify responsibilities to examine, not a placement rate. We invent none to rank the candidates. [2]

An employment measure must specify permanent work, self-employment and short jobs. It must address graduates whose situation cannot be verified. Counting only respondents can hide many people lost to follow-up.

For Emile, those rules should precede support payments. For Lumarque, past cohorts’ results could test his experience. The comparison requires public follow-up of young people’s opportunities, without treating every diploma or career as automatic proof of success.
