Munir Mourra vs Joanas Gué: credit needs a harvest that can reach a market

A proposed agricultural bank and a past canal-repair account connect finance, water and maintenance in one policy question.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A loan can buy seed without bringing rain. A canal can bring water while a farmer lacks planting money. Agricultural comparison should examine whether the pieces connect rather than count institutions and construction separately.

Munir Mourra’s MPH proposes an agricultural bank, canal works and agricultural distribution. This is a party programme, not evidence of an already operating bank or resolved harvest risk. [1]

In an interview reported in January 2009, Joanas Gué said emergency funds repaired 115 irrigation systems after the 2008 hurricanes. This is the former minister’s account, not our verification of each system. He also criticized limited agricultural lending. [2]

Together, the records expose connected needs: finance before planting, water when crops need it and usable routes to market. Repayment should fit the season. That is our assessment, not verified terms already adopted by MPH’s proposed bank.

For Gué, repair follow-up would demonstrate lessons from experience. For Mourra, a costed first project and lending rules would explain the proposal. Neither a historical total nor a new bank’s name proves the next harvest safer.
