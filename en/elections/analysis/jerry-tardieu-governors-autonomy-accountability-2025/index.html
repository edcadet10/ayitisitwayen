Jerry Tardieu and governors: autonomy still needs accountability

The 2025 dispute reaches beyond the word “federal.” It concerns who manages departmental money and who can demand accounts.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On May 26, 2025, Juno7 reported Jerry Tardieu’s criticism of proposed departmental governors in the draft constitution. He warned of rivalry with the president, unequal departmental resources and expensive administration. He characterized the direction as federalist. That was his political interpretation. [1]

In the CEP-hosted copy of the May 2025 draft, article 68 gives departments administrative and financial autonomy. Article 68-2 provides for governors elected for five years with unlimited reelection. That supplies a concrete basis for debating concentration of power at departmental level. [2]

Autonomy is not written as an absence of scrutiny. Article 68-6 requires collaboration with the central administration on development plans. Article 68-8 requires accountability to the departmental assembly and reporting to the central administration. The text also gives the Court of Auditors a role in grounds for removal. [2]

This draft is not presented as law in force. Reading it sharpens the question: would its checks work, and what rules would keep poorer departments from falling behind? Scrutinizing Tardieu’s position requires his alternative for resource-sharing and local accountability. Retaining a unitary state does not, by itself, answer those questions.
