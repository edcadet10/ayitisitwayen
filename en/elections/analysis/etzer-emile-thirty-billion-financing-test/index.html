Etzer Emile’s $30 billion plan: who provides the money?

A total shows the scale of a promise. Funding sources show who carries the risk.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Etzer Emile’s campaign website presents a US$30 billion programme over five years. Its infrastructure page assigns US$15 billion to that sector: half the total. These are proposed envelopes, not funds we have verified as available. [1] [2]

Dividing 30 billion by five gives an average of US$6 billion a year. That is our calculation from the stated total. It does not imply equal annual spending; a large project may require more funding at the start. [1]

A more important distinction follows. A grant differs from a loan the state must repay. Private investment may come with tariff or profit conditions. Tax revenue requires a collection plan. Grouping these under “funding” can hide choices households will live with.

A financing table should give each project’s cost in gourdes and dollars, confirmed sources, unfunded amount and resulting debt. Without it, a reader cannot distinguish ambition from funded commitments.

The size of the number alone makes a plan neither sound nor impossible. Funding, timing and service must fit together. A completed road without maintenance can become another bill for the same public.
