The votes behind Tardieu’s terrorist-financing announcement

A transmission letter separates the first chamber’s vote, the Senate’s vote and publication of the law.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On September 8, 2016, Tardieu’s account celebrated a law against terrorist financing being voted and said he belonged to its supporting special commission. A scanned legislative letter helps place that announcement in sequence. [1]

The October 6 letter records a lower-house first reading on September 7 and a Senate second reading on September 28. The retained mirror’s cover identifies publication in Le Moniteur on October 13. The instrument amends the November 2013 money-laundering and terrorist-financing law. [2]

Those dates make the September 8 announcement consistent with the lower-house vote. They do not make it evidence that both chambers had finished that day. Nor would the later Senate date, by itself, disprove that an earlier chamber vote occurred.

This review read selected scanned pages, not the complete current legal framework, and could not compare the mirror’s bytes with the original publisher’s copy. The letter names chamber leaders transmitting the text. It supplies no individual roll call or independent proof of Tardieu’s commission role.

The electoral question is what his contribution changed: the wording, safeguards, oversight or enforcement. Commission records and voting lists would clarify responsibility; implementation records would show results. A public claim of legislative work deserves that follow-through, rather than either automatic credit or an invented contradiction.
