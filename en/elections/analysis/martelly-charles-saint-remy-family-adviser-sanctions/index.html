Martelly and Charles Saint-Rémy: three links to keep distinct

Family, an advisory role and sanctions are three different facts. Putting them in order makes questions about power more precise.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A January 2023 Reuters report describes Charles Saint-Rémy as former president Michel Martelly’s brother-in-law and adviser. The link extends beyond a surname to a reported political role. The report does not provide a written mandate defining that adviser’s powers. [1]

On January 13, 2023, Canada announced sanctions against Saint-Rémy, identifying a businessman and Martelly associate. It said it had reason to believe those targeted protected gang activity, including drug trafficking and corruption. Those are Canada’s stated grounds for an administrative measure. [2]

The regulations current to October 19, 2026 still list Saint-Rémy in the significant-corruption category. That list is not a criminal judgment. It does not, by itself, establish that Martelly ordered or received money from a gang operation. [3]

The public question becomes clearer when each link is traced separately. What access came with family ties? Which files did he influence as an adviser? As a businessman, did he take part in decisions involving his interests? Each answer needs its own records: appointments, meetings, contracts or transactions.

We have not established Saint-Rémy’s role in Martelly’s current presidential campaign. Serious scrutiny therefore asks who has access to the candidate today without turning kinship into an automatic accusation. Informal influence deserves questions even when it comes without an office or official title.
