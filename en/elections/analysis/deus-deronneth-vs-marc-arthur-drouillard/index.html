Déus Deronneth vs Marc-Arthur Drouillard: a result is not the whole election story

One obtained a changed result after challenges. The other announced withdrawal to support Jovenel Moïse. These are different political acts to examine.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

A vote table can obscure how a candidate reached his position. Déus Deronneth’s and Marc-Arthur Drouillard’s 2016 records show why decisions and alliances must be read alongside totals.

The EU observers’ report says examination of 34 legislative challenges reversed one deputy result: Marigot. In January 2017, Rezo Nòdwès reported that the final decision gave PONT candidate Deronneth victory over Dieudonné Lhérisson, who led in preliminary results. The article is not the electoral judgment itself. [1] [2]

For Drouillard, the same EU report records his September 27, 2016 withdrawal in favour of Jovenel Moïse. Its provisional-results annex gives Drouillard 978 votes. Both can be true: announcing withdrawal does not automatically remove a name from every electoral material or prevent votes for it. [1]

Deronneth’s record calls for the documents and rules that changed the result. Drouillard’s calls for an account of his endorsement and how he assesses it afterward. Winning a challenge is not automatically proof of fraud; endorsing a candidate is not participation in others’ later acts. The provisional 978 votes do not measure Drouillard’s support today.

The comparison concerns how they compete and accept the contest’s rules. Voters need accessible decisions and dated alliances so a single figure does not stand in for an entire career.
