Hans Joseph vs Kely Bastien: transparency should leave a record

One led ULCC, the other the Senate. Their experience calls for different records to assess what they did.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Transparency is easy to pronounce into a microphone. It becomes useful when a citizen can find a decision, expenditure or case follow-up. Hans Joseph and Kely Bastien offer experience against which to ask that question in different institutions.

A 2011 trip report on the U.S. Senate Foreign Relations Committee’s website presents Bastien, a former Haitian Senate president, with objectives of transparency and better relations between branches. These are reported objectives, not audited achievements. [1]

In the Badio statement reproduced by Juno7 in July 2021, ULCC gives dismissal and complaint dates concerning its former employee. Hans Joseph had led ULCC since 2020. The statement is not the complete disciplinary file or proof of his knowledge of an assassination plot. [2] [3]

For Bastien, useful records include assembly budgets, spending and decisions. For Joseph, they include decision records and complaint follow-up while protecting lawful investigations. Scrutiny need not expose witnesses or information that endangers people.

This comparison does not declare one cleaner. It identifies something stronger than a title: what record each left for public examination. A candidate asking for trust can begin with responsibilities already held.
