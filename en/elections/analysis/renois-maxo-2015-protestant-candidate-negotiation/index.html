Renois and Maxo Joseph: what the 2015 Protestant candidate selection meant

An agreement among leaders selected a candidate; it did not transfer citizens’ votes.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In September 2015, a facilitation committee led by pastor Gérald Bataille selected Jean Clarens Renois to represent the Protestant sector, according to Rezo Nòdwès. Maxo Joseph was among seven candidates participating in the agreement to select one person. [1]

The report describes two negotiating stages. The first retained three names: Renois, Jean-Chavannes Jeune and Jean Palème Mathurin. Discussions then produced the Renois choice. This records how leaders sought to consolidate several candidacies behind one. [1]

It does not establish that every church or Protestant voter participated. Nor does it prove all the other candidates removed their names from the ballot. Signing an agreement, endorsing someone and withdrawing from an election are different actions. [1]

Understanding a candidate’s political base requires examining networks that help mobilize support: parties, associations, churches and other groups. No network owns its members’ votes. People can share a faith and make different political choices.

This history helps explain Renois’s and Maxo Joseph’s political paths without inventing an alliance automatically continuing from 2015. Present support requires a current statement or commitment. An old agreement belongs in their record; it is not a political blank check without an expiry date. [2]
