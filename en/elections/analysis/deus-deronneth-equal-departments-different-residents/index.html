Deronneth’s equal departmental grant buys unequal amounts per resident

A population-based calculation reveals the choice hidden inside an apparently equal split.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-18

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

In Deronneth’s April 2024 budget model, each of Haiti’s ten departments would receive 3.20645 billion gourdes. That is one-tenth of the 10% departmental allocation, calculated from the initial 2023–2024 state budget. [1] [2] [3]

IHSI estimates 4,038,816 residents in Ouest and 403,045 in Nippes for 2024. Dividing the same proposed grant by those populations gives about 794 gourdes per resident in Ouest and 7,956 in Nippes: roughly ten times as much per person. [4] [1]

This is an illustrative calculation combining his earlier model with a later-published estimate. It covers only the departmental component, not all public resources reaching either place, and describes no actual payment. The population figures are estimates, not a new census.

Equal amounts for territories and equal amounts for residents are different political choices. A smaller department can still face fixed costs: maintaining offices, roads or a service network. A larger one can face heavier demand. The calculation identifies a trade-off; it cannot decide fairness by itself.

Deronneth should explain which costs his departmental grant is meant to cover and why every department receives the same amount. Would population, poverty, distance or existing infrastructure change the formula? A transparent answer would let voters compare a flat grant with a base amount plus a needs-based share.
