Daniest Adrien vs Ricardo Jean Pierre: a party slate is not its vote total

PRC has more Senate entries; PANAH has one more deputy entry. The data show participation in the contest without predicting who will win.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Before describing a candidate as strong or weak, we need to say what is being measured. For Daniest Adrien and Ricardo Jean Pierre, the CEP registry provides a checkable comparison: other registrations carrying their party codes.

Adrien appears under PRC; Jean Pierre under PANAH. In the reconciled collection of 11,142 entries, PRC has seven Senate and eight Chamber of Deputies entries. PANAH has three Senate and nine deputy entries. We count exact codes, not people presumed to be their allies. [1]

The comparison changes with the office. PRC has four more Senate entries; PANAH has one more deputy entry. These calculations use the same snapshot. They establish neither seats won nor a president’s parliamentary majority. The CEP has not finished approving these candidacies, and each constituency has its own voters. [1]

These data matter because a presidential programme may require laws and decisions at other levels of government. Registration is not a commitment to vote together on every law. Agreements with other parties may exist outside this count. This record does not support inventing either candidate’s programme, financing or family history. The next test is obtaining the same documents from both while preserving the differences already established.
