Deus Deronneth: waiting for security, but until when?

His criticism of changing electoral rules raises a real difficulty: protecting voters while keeping the transition time-bound.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On October 10, 2026, Deus Deronneth criticized repeated revisions to electoral rules. Rezo Nòdwès reports that he sought a realistic security-restoration timetable before fixing the election calendar, alongside a defined duration, objectives and measurable results for Alix Didier Fils-Aimé’s government. [1]

Two needs are intertwined. A polling date does little for someone unable to reach a station safely. Yet “security first” without defined criteria allows authorities to say conditions are never ready. A principle meant to protect an election can become a reason for indefinite delay.

A useful assessment would specify the conditions required to open polling stations, who verifies them and the arrangements for displaced voters. These are proposed evaluation criteria, not a plan attributed to Deronneth.

Deronneth’s criticism is strongest where it connects time to results. The same standard should apply to any alternative transition he supports: a defined mandate, public milestones and accountability for missed commitments. Replacing personnel does not resolve an unclear limit on power.
