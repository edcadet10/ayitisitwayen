Dominique Dupuy vs Savannah Savary: the same precision, different rules for money

A diplomatic-spending decision and an account of family businesses are different records. They help identify what the public needs to assess each candidate.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-16

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

Accountability does not mean demanding exactly the same papers from everyone. A minister uses public resources; an entrepreneur has private interests that may intersect with state decisions. Dominique Dupuy’s and Savannah Savary’s records show how the same requirement for precision can require different documents.

In July 2024, Dupuy defended diplomatic recalls as reform to align expenditure with Haiti’s means. Métropole reported that more than 80 diplomats and representation staff were reportedly affected. She did not publish the list during that appearance, emphasizing competence and rotation while rejecting political or factional motives. [1]

In an October 2026 Haitian Times interview, Savary describes inheriting Distillerie 1928 and Habitation Robert and says they employ more than 100 people. She declined financial details, citing fear of becoming a target. That is her account, not our audit of employment, debt or assets. [2]

For Dupuy, recalls do not measure savings. Assessment needs expenditure before and after, replacements and consular service. For Savary, employment does not measure personal wealth; assessment needs economic interests that could affect public decisions and safeguards against that influence. Security concerns do not erase those questions, but require responsible disclosure.

The comparison must not invent savings from a headcount or state favour from a family business. It follows what each candidate had authority to decide and the records those decisions leave. That lets readers examine public spending and private interests without conflating them.
