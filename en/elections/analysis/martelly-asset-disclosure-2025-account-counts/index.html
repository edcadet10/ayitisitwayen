Martelly’s asset disclosures: why 20 plus 17 does not automatically mean 37

ULCC’s allegations require precise scrutiny. Accounts counted at different times are not necessarily different accounts.

Ayiti Sitwayen · Updated: 2026-11-15

An examination of documented information, with interpretations clearly identified.

On December 8, 2025, RHINEWS reported ULCC findings of delays, omissions and discrepancies between Michel and Sophia Martelly’s disclosures and banking records. It says ULCC recommended proceedings against the former president for false asset disclosure. An investigative recommendation is not a conviction. [1]

The report describes 20 additional accounts and a credit card omitted at entry, and 17 active accounts and three additional cards at exit. It also mentions campaign- and Fondation Rose et Blanc-related accounts. These should not all be presented as Michel Martelly’s personal accounts. [1]

Adding 20 and 17 cannot establish 37 distinct accounts without comparing the lists: one account may appear at both dates. Accounts, credit cards and monetary balances are also different units. An account count alone establishes neither total wealth nor illicit funds.

That does not lessen the need to examine the allegations. It identifies the verification sequence: establish ownership, compare required and actual disclosure, and let adjudication determine responsibility. Records involving a couple or foundation require precise attribution rather than transferring every allegation across a family.
