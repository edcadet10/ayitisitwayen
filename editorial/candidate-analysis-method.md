# Candidate analysis and opinion

The Elections section links to `/elections/analysis/` and its English counterpart.
These pages and the revised About page are generated by `build-editorial.py`.
The landing pages and relevant candidate profiles link back to original pieces.

## Reporting and writing

Every article answers one concrete question in language a sixteen-year-old can
understand. Establish the event, people, money and sequence before procedural
detail. Explain institutions when first named. Do not fabricate scenes, interviews
or a reader’s personal circumstances. The maximum is 500 words per language,
including headline and deck; references and shared navigation are separate.
Split larger subjects into linked articles with distinct findings.

Analysis interprets sourced records. Opinion makes an explicitly labeled
editorial argument. Both require accurate factual foundations. The article kind
is visible on cards and article pages and appears in structured metadata.

Study of ProPublica, ICIJ, Reuters, AP and InSight Crime informed the narrative
structure: a clear central event, a traceable chain of evidence, understandable
stakes, and competing explanations tested against records. Their prose is not
copied. The source register records which examples were actually accessible.

## Evidence and public-interest relationships

Prefer original judgments, court orders, laws, audit reports, regulatory filings,
sanctions instruments and official financial records. Preserve the URL, issuing
institution, date, relevant page or paragraph and a local fingerprint. A search
result, machine-generated summary or name match is a lead, not an established
finding. Read the surrounding passage and test contrary explanations.

Distinguish allegation, testimony, administrative designation and judicial
finding. Follow appeals, dismissals, releases and other subsequent decisions.
Do not present an old indictment as current after it was overturned. A civil
decision is not a criminal conviction. Record the scope of the docket actually
reviewed; do not claim access to all proceedings or private evidence.

For indirect relationships, document each edge separately: person, institution,
business, family relationship, appointment, payment or alleged assistance, with
dates and source. Explain the public-interest relevance. Shared surnames,
photographs, employment or family relationships alone do not establish misconduct.
Exclude private contact details, unique personal identifiers and minors’ private
lives. Do not infer guilt across a relationship chain.

Check people with similar names before attaching a record. In particular, Jude
Célestin is not plaintiff Odilon S. Celestin; Claude Joseph is not John Joël Joseph;
Jean Ernest Muscadin is not André Muscadin; Charles Saint-Rémy is not Pierre-Léon
Junior Saint Remy; Christian Emmanuel Sanon is not every candidate named Sanon.
Never label a candidate cleared because a corpus search returned no match.

The existing directory’s source-preview presentation is preserved. Original
editorial articles additionally link their cited primary documents directly from
the bibliography so readers can inspect the evidence. No third-party document
viewer or automatic publisher fetch is embedded.

## The first school-fee series

The sources are BRH’s 2011 annual report, Haiti’s audit court’s 2017–2018 and
2023–2024 reports, U.S. opinions in appeal 20-1412 and district case 18-CV-7340,
and FNE’s own later beneficiary list. The reviewed opinions are dated 2022,
2023 and 2025; this is not a representation of a complete current docket.

The 148-page official 2017–2018 PDF differs in layout from the earlier
150-page university mirror. The public article cites the official version:
FNE section pages 138–142, table 59 on page 140. Totals were checked against the
visible table: USD 120,130,740.50 collected, USD 117,760,594.00 transferred to the
Treasury, USD 2,370,146.50 remaining. The difference is the reported balance,
not evidence of a missing sum or a payment to a president.

The newer budget report counts FNE resources but omits its expenditure from the
reported government balance. This is an accounting criticism covering a later
fiscal year, not a finding that a named former president took the money.
FNE’s 2025–2026 list is self-reported and contains inconsistent dates; it is not
treated as independently audited or summed as complete annual expenditure.

## Review, audio and discovery

The Creole copy receives an independent blind reading before that reviewer opens
English, followed by a fidelity comparison. This is not native-human
certification or a human-reader comprehension study. Changes are checked against
the actual source and applied to the saved text. Reuse review of unchanged copy.

Ayiti Sitwayen is the responsible publisher. Narration is identified as synthetic.
Do not invent staff credentials, interviews, fieldwork,
confidential sources or intelligence access. Contact links accept corrections;
do not claim that a request for comment was sent unless it actually was.

Audio references are tied to the exact Creole article and MP3 hashes. Controls
require user playback and provide the native text as a transcript. English pages
label the recording as Haitian Creole. Verify file format, duration, loading and
critical names, amounts and negation before describing audio quality.

Canonical URLs, reciprocal language links, Article metadata, visible dates,
native candidate links and sitemap entries support crawling. Verify the actual
deployed HTML, media and responses. Google eligibility, a crawl request, actual
indexing and search ranking are separate states; report only the observed state.
